# Design thesis — Deep End v2.1

devShark feels like a modern learning field journal discovered at the edge of the ocean: disciplined editorial typography, tactile paper surfaces, charted paths, technical diagrammatic marks, and a restrained shark-fin signature. The interface is serious enough to trust, playful enough to revisit, and focused enough to support real learning.

## Product behavior

- devShark is a developer-learning product. Its language is technical and practical; its visual rhythm can use modules, code structure, and system diagrams without turning into a fake terminal.
- The accent identifies the product. It does not wash entire pages or replace hierarchy.
- The waterline marks progress, transitions, and the brand. Fins are signatures, not stickers.
- Real content, progress, room data, rankings, and authored counts are the primary visual material.

## Density

- Public discovery: medium density, clear editorial hierarchy, one primary action per section.
- Quiz, Learn, Profile, Leaderboard: medium-to-high density with stable geometry.
- `/dev`: high operational density, minimal decorative motion, explicit status and safe actions.
- Use fewer, stronger containers. Avoid rounded card nesting and marketing-scale heroes inside operational routes.

## Brand character

The system is intelligent, curious, crafted, fair, calm, structured, tactile, editorial, focused, and quietly playful. It is not childish, casino-like, cyberpunk, generic SaaS, generic AI, or decorative at the expense of comprehension.

Ocean language is occasional. Use a waterline, fin, or understated phrase when it clarifies identity. Do not force “dive,” “splash,” “attack,” or “feeding” language into every action.

## Trust contract

Show real authored counts, what the free tier opens and what Premium opens (the price with VAT included), fair rankings, curated-answer authority, question reporting, privacy controls, and account deletion. Do not invent testimonials, partner logos, ratings, activity, success percentages, classrooms, users, discounts, or deadlines.

The trust contract promised free-forever access until the owner made devShark freemium on 25 September 2026 (`SECOND-HANDOFF-25-9-2026.md`).

## Decision test

A new design choice belongs only if it improves at least one of: orientation, comprehension, confidence, continuity, accessibility, or topic recognition. Decoration that does none of those is removed.
