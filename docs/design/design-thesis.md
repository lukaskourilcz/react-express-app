# Design thesis — Deep End v2.1

StudyShark and devShark feel like a modern learning field journal discovered at the edge of the ocean: disciplined editorial typography, tactile paper surfaces, charted paths, subject-specific scientific and cultural marks, and a restrained shark-fin signature. The interface is serious enough to trust, playful enough to revisit, and focused enough to support real learning.

## Product behavior

- StudyShark is a broad general-learning ecosystem. Its six subjects share navigation, progress conventions, and shell behavior while gaining identity from real topic content, glyphs, and distinct diagrammatic chapter plates.
- devShark is a sibling developer-learning product. Its language is technical and practical; its visual rhythm can use modules, code structure, and system diagrams without turning into a fake terminal.
- The subject accent identifies context. It does not wash entire pages or replace hierarchy.
- The waterline marks progress, transitions, and brand family. Fins are signatures, not stickers.
- Real content, progress, room data, rankings, and authored counts are the primary visual material.

## Density

- Public discovery: medium density, clear editorial hierarchy, one primary action per section.
- Quiz, Learn, Profile, Leaderboard: medium-to-high density with stable geometry.
- `/dev`: high operational density, minimal decorative motion, explicit status and safe actions.
- Use fewer, stronger containers. Avoid rounded card nesting and marketing-scale heroes inside operational routes.

## Brand character

The system is intelligent, curious, crafted, fair, calm, structured, tactile, editorial, focused, and quietly playful. It is not childish, casino-like, cyberpunk, generic SaaS, generic AI, or decorative at the expense of comprehension.

Ocean language is occasional. Use a waterline, fin, or understated phrase when it clarifies identity. Do not force “dive,” “splash,” “attack,” or “feeding” language into every action.

## Trust contract

Show real authored counts, free-forever access, fair subject-scoped rankings, curated-answer authority, optional post-answer AI, question reporting, bilingual support, privacy controls, and account deletion. Do not invent testimonials, partner logos, ratings, activity, success percentages, classrooms, users, or supporter benefits.

## Decision test

A new design choice belongs only if it improves at least one of: orientation, comprehension, confidence, continuity, accessibility, or subject recognition. Decoration that does none of those is removed.
