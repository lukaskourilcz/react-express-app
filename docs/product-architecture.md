# Product, brand, and deployment architecture

This repository implements one product, **devShark**: an English-only
developer-learning app whose one subject is `webdev`. It runs as one
React/Vite client, twelve Vercel handlers under `api/` and one Supabase
database. Every scored server flow still validates the subject before it
selects questions or writes progress, because progress, XP, coins and the
database are keyed by subject.

StudyShark, the general-subject product, lives in its own repository
(`lukaskourilcz/studyshark`). Nothing here builds, links to or serves it. The
database's subject checks still accept its six subjects; the API's subject
scope refuses them.

## Sources of truth

- `client/product-catalog.ts` owns the product name, SEO copy, product
  resolution and the outside URLs: `SOCIAL_PROFILES`, `MERCH_SHOP` (the
  Spreadshop shop and items) and the trader details in `TRADER`.
  `resolveCatalogProductId` returns devShark for an unset environment and
  throws for any other product or subject lock, so a build configured for
  another product fails instead of shipping devShark under its name.
- `client/src/lib/products.ts` attaches presentation data to that catalog.
- `shared/subject-catalog.ts` owns the security-sensitive subject-to-topic and
  subject-to-category mapping shared by browser and server code, and the
  landing's question and coding-challenge counts (2,134 and 769), which
  `npm run test:launch` recomputes.
- `client/src/lib/subjects.ts` adds subject presentation tokens without
  redefining category ownership.
- `lib/product-scope.ts` exposes the server's subject and category allow-list,
  which is always `webdev`.
- `shared/coding-catalog.ts` owns the coding task types, technique groups,
  difficulty ladder, badge conditions and GitHub garden paths.
  `lib/coding/catalog.ts` builds the catalogue from the tasks under
  `lib/coding/tasks/` and stays on the server with the reference solutions
  under `lib/coding/solutions/`. The browser reads only
  `shared/coding-index.ts` (summaries, regenerated by
  `npm run build:coding-index`).
- `shared/tiers.ts` is the only statement of what the free tier includes and
  what Premium opens ("Tiers and billing").
- `shared/learn-sections.ts` groups the Learn topics and the FDE path into
  three sections ("Learn sections").

`api/` holds exactly twelve physical handlers: `admin/[op].ts`,
`play/[action].ts`, `quiz/challenge.ts`, `quiz/daily.ts`, `quiz/questions.ts`,
`quiz/roadmap.ts`, `quiz/submit.ts`, `user/[op].ts`, `flashcards.ts`,
`health.ts`, `leaderboard.ts` and `settings.ts`. A new endpoint is a
`resource=` or `op=` branch inside one of them; `npm run test:launch` fails
when the count changes.

devShark ships English only (owner decision, 21 October 2026). Every task,
lesson and question text is a plain English string, and no request or
response carries a language.

Do not introduce another product, subject, brand array, URL map or category
ownership map. Extend these sources instead.

## Daily-habit layer

Spaced mastery, the Today queue, forgiving streaks, badges, Shark Cards, the
study advisor and adaptive placement change no product or subject scope.
Their shared sources are `shared/mastery.ts` (cleared and mastered state and
spaced-review scheduling), `shared/badges.ts` (the server-verifiable badge
catalog; the API and the coding badges use it, and the Profile shows no badge
section) and `shared/shark-cards.ts` (where a saved question's "Learn this
topic" link goes). Their endpoints are `resource=`/`op=` branches inside the
existing handlers, and their storage starts in
`supabase/supabase-schema-024.sql`. Shark Cards and badges never affect
access, content, XP, scores, streaks or ranks.

Streak protection is the one bounded exception, written out in
`shared/rewards.ts`: two a month free, extras bought with coins earned from
verified learning (no cash price), a ceiling that never rises above two, and
an effect limited to the day count of a streak. No board ranks by streak: the
answer boards rank by correct answers and accuracy, and "This month" by
verified XP.

A streak day is a UTC day with verified learning, not only a quiz.
`advance_verified_streak` is the one place a day is counted, and four routines
call it behind the receipts that make them idempotent:
`record_verified_quiz_result_v2` (a quiz or daily result),
`complete_verified_roadmap_attempt` (a Learn level or part test with every
question answered, passed or not), `record_coding_verdict` (any passing
verdict, in the coding section or inside a Learn level) and
`record_challenge_completion` (a Biggest Shark Challenge run that earned XP).
A level ended by running out of hearts is closed by `api/quiz/roadmap.ts`
without the completion routine, so that request calls
`advance_verified_streak` directly: a Learn level counts however it ends. A
second source the same day adds nothing, and a replayed receipt moves
nothing. `user_stats.last_quiz_date` keeps its name and means the last UTC day
with verified learning.

Missed days are bridged first by the shield, then by the month's
protections. A shield covers two UTC dates, the one it was raised on and the
next: `activate_streak_shield` ends it at 00:00 UTC the day after tomorrow.
`user_streak_freezes.shield_days` keeps every date a shield covered in the
last 40 days, so a second shield never turns the first one's days into missed
days. `streak_day_shielded`, `streak_missed_days` and `streak_live` hold that
one rule, used by `advance_verified_streak`, `activate_streak_shield`,
`friend_list`, `settle_coin_milestones` and, in the same terms, `liveStreak` on
the Profile. `activate_streak_shield` refuses and spends nothing when a missed
day still needs the protection the shield would take: the handler answers 409
`shield_would_end_streak` ("Learn today to keep your streak"), and the Profile
does not offer the shield then. The month change restores the two
protections and never clears a shield (`refresh_streak_freezes`). When the
Profile cannot read the protections, it shows a streak that depends on them
as "—" rather than 0.

The server keeps UTC days. The streak card, Today's target, the done and
empty panels, the daily challenge and the cleared-level tooltip say when the
day changes on the learner's clock (`client/src/lib/utcDay.ts`). Today's plan
and the Leaderboard's Today board move on to the new day at 00:00 UTC and
when the learner returns to the tab (`useUtcDay`), so a page left open
overnight does not keep yesterday's target or board. Reaching Today's target
hands out nothing: the panel says the plan is complete and when the next day
starts.

### Question of the day

`/daily` and `/daily/<date>` show one public question a day. The track is a
pure function of the date (`shared/daily-question.ts`, fifteen served
categories in rotation from `QOTD_EPOCH`), so the build writes each day's page
head and share image (`client/src/og/ogImages.ts`) ahead of the day, as it does
for every coding task. `client/src/lib/routeHead.ts` reads such a prerendered
head before the first route writes one, so a dated page or a task page keeps
its own title, description and canonical URL. The Coding home and its track
pages (`/coding`, `/coding/<track>`, `/coding/fullstack`) get a head the same
way and are in the sitemap; the task pages are not. `/coding/review` names
`/coding` as canonical and stays out. `/coding/system-design` and every
address under it are one prerendered not-found page, `noindex` and with no
canonical (`vercel.json` sends them all there), because system design is
hidden.

The question comes from `GET /api/quiz/daily?qotd=<date|today>`
(`lib/daily-question.ts`, inside the daily handler): a seeded pick from that
track in the served bank, options shuffled by the same seed, the answer
sealed in a quiz session with scope `qotd`. A future date answers 404
`qotd_not_yet`, so nobody reads tomorrow's question today.
`api/quiz/submit.ts` grades a `qotd` session as if signed out, whoever sends
it: no result receipt, XP, streak day, leaderboard entry or concept-review
record. Every read seals a fresh session and one check claims
it, so the response is `private, no-store` and the page fetches it with
`cache: 'no-store'`. After a claimed or expired session, "Load it again"
starts the question over.

### Daily challenge

`GET /api/quiz/daily` gives everyone the same five questions for the UTC day.
A signed-in learner's session carries one attempt id for the day
(`stableAttemptId('daily', user, subject, date)`), and `api/quiz/submit.ts`
ranks only that attempt: a daily fetched signed out and submitted signed in is
graded as practice, with no receipt. The recorded time runs from the first
time the learner got the day's questions, kept as a `daily-start:<attempt>`
marker row in `quiz_submissions` and sealed into every later session of the
day, so fetching again just before submitting does not shorten it. The same
lookup tells the client the day is already played. The response is
`private, no-store`, because the session inside it belongs to one caller.
`/quiz?mode=daily` starts the daily challenge once the sign-in is known and
then drops the parameter; the Home "Daily challenge" tile and the empty Today
leaderboard link there.

A daily fetched signed in also names that account (`userId` in the session).
`api/quiz/submit.ts` refuses it with 401 `sign_in_required` when the submit
carries no credentials and with 409 `session_owner_mismatch` when another
account sends it, both before anything is claimed, so the day's one attempt is
never spent as a guest's. `api/quiz/submit.ts` grades only the sessions it
serves (quiz, review, daily, question of the day, challenge batch,
assessment); it refuses a Learn session.

## Leaderboards

`/leaderboard` opens on the last 30 days, so a new learner can reach the top.
"This month", all time and Today (the daily challenge) are the other tabs.

**What counts.** `user_activity_days` (user, UTC day, category, correct,
answered) feeds the 30-day and all-time boards. Three verified routines write
it and nothing else does: `record_verified_quiz_result_v2` for quizzes and the
daily challenge, `record_roadmap_answer_v2` for a signed-in learner's first
answer to a Learn question, and `record_challenge_completion` for a finished
Biggest Shark Challenge run. Each write sits behind the receipt that makes its
routine idempotent. A question counts at most once per learner and UTC day,
in a quiz, a Learn level or a Challenge run, and a Learn answer counts only
while its level or part test is not yet passed. Coding passes are not
answers and do not count. Quiz history from before 26 September 2026 lives
only in `user_category_stats`; the boards do not read it.

`window_leaderboard` and `window_leaderboard_rank` rank correct answers, then
fewer answers for the same number correct, and equal results share a rank.
The all-time boards (`subject_leaderboard`, `category_leaderboard`) read the
same rows with no date window and the same five-answer minimum. Today
(`daily_leaderboard_v2`) ranks by correct answers alone; the time is shown
but decides nothing. The all-time boards and Today arrive in order without a
rank, and the Leaderboard screen numbers them so equal results share a rank
there too.

**XP for a repeated question.** A correct quiz answer pays 2 + 2 × difficulty
XP (`api/quiz/submit.ts`), and pays again once the account's last XP for that
question is at least 60 minutes old. `user_question_xp` holds that hour;
`question_xp_claim` claims each correct answer's question in one upsert, so
two results paying one question at once pay it once. The daily challenge
keeps its minimum of 20. A Challenge run pays 5 XP a correct answer, under
the same hour. A repeat earns XP and coins and never a place on a board.
`record_verified_quiz_result_v3` and `record_challenge_completion_v2` also
return the correct answers whose hour held their XP back (`xpHeld`), and the
browser says so after the gain, on the learner's own clock ("No XP for 2
answers this time: you can earn it again from 14:32, an hour after you last
earned it."). The awarded XP is kept on the receipt row
(`quiz_attempts.quest_xp`), and the stats handler credits coins for that
amount once per attempt, also when a retry finds the result already
recorded.

**This month** (`period=month`) ranks the current calendar month (UTC) by the
verified XP each learner earned in it; the month's top three are paid coins
on this ranking ("Coins"). `user_xp_days` holds that XP per learner, UTC day
and subject, and only `add_xp_day` writes it, inside each verified award's
own transaction: `record_verified_quiz_result_v2`,
`record_verified_activity_xp` (a Challenge run, a coding pass and its repeat
XP, a Learn step's repeat XP) and `complete_verified_roadmap_attempt` (a Learn
level or part test passed for the first time, at `learn_step_xp`, the numbers
of `shared/progression.ts`). Guest XP merged at sign-in (`merge_user_xp`) does
not count, because nothing verified it. A launch contract fails if a routine
adds to `user_xp` without adding to the ledger. `month_xp_ranks` is the one
ranking (`RANK()`: 1, 1, 3); `month_xp_leaderboard` and
`month_xp_leaderboard_rank` serve the board and the learner's own line. The
screen pins the learner's own line under the list and has no topic filter,
since XP is not counted per topic. Below 480px the period control uses short
labels ("30 days", "Month", "All time", "Today") so the four fit at 320px.

**Names and photos.** Every board names a learner through
`board_display_name(user_id)`. Profile's `leaderboard-name` operation
chooses `nickname` (the default, the sharkname) or `account`: a name from
the verified provider identity or the local part of a confirmed email, never
the full email or browser-editable `user_metadata`. This choice does not
change what friends see. `user_stats.show_on_leaderboards`, set through
`leaderboard-visibility`, controls only the optional public photo; no
setting removes a name from a board.

**Serving.** `api/leaderboard.ts` serves every shared board (`period=30d`,
`month`, `global`, `category` and `daily`) to everyone with
`public, s-maxage=60` and no `stale-while-revalidate`, so the CDN never serves
a board older than a minute. A request with a Bearer token or `me=1` also gets
the learner's own line, takes the `leaderboardPersonal` bucket and is answered
`private, no-store`. A `limit` is served as the next of 10, 25, 50, 100 or 200
rows, and a read that reaches the function takes the address read bucket
("Sign-in, caching and limits"). Today's `date` must be one calendar day
(`YYYY-MM-DD`, year 0001 or later), sent once; anything else answers 400.

`friend_list` orders friends by correct answers and accuracy, shows each
friend's live streak by the streak rule, and marks a friend active today when
their last verified learning day is today.

### Biggest Shark Challenge

Every Challenge question allows 90 seconds. The learner picks topics before
starting, and the run seals them; refills cannot change them. A completed run
with a positive score saves itself under one run id, for a signed-in learner
under the server identity above and for a guest under a generated name tied
to the run. There is no name-entry form, and a zero score creates no score or
guest name. A run already saved with another score, owner or topic set
answers 409 `run_already_saved`, and the browser drops it from the results it
still has to send. The board shows a count of the run's topics with the
topics in a tooltip, or a gold fin for a run over every available topic.

A known limit: a run submits its answer proofs from the client
(`api/quiz/challenge.ts`), and without a server-side append-only attempt
ledger a determined client can still leave out earlier proof batches. Do not
claim the record is complete; a full fix has to fit an existing handler.

### XP again for a replayed Learn step

A Learn level or part test pays its learning XP on its first pass: the XP is
derived from verified progress (`learn_step_xp`) and is not in `user_xp`. A
signed-in pass of a step already passed pays that XP again when the step last
paid the account at least 60 minutes ago. `api/quiz/roadmap.ts` completes the
attempt with `complete_verified_roadmap_attempt_v2`, which runs
`complete_verified_roadmap_attempt` unchanged (mastery, spaced review, the
streak and the first-pass XP) and then claims the step in
`user_learn_step_xp`. The claim is one guarded UPDATE under the attempt and
progress row locks, so two completions at once pay once
(`supabase/tests/231`). A repeat is XP only, under its own award id
(`learn-repeat:<attempt>`), into the balance and the month's ledger through
`record_verified_activity_xp`: no coins, no badge and no first-pass reward. A
pass inside the hour, a failed replay and a guest's pass pay nothing. Part
test n pays 300 × n each time, so part test 3 can pay 900 XP once an hour.

The completion's `repeatXp` says what the pass paid again (`xp`), or that the
hour held it back (`withheld: 'cooldown'`), and when the step's XP opens
(`availableAt`). The browser toasts "+N XP again" and syncs the balance, or
says when the XP opens again, on the learner's own clock (`XpToaster`).

### Sharknames and what friends see

A learner's handle is their sharkname: the address friends add them by, 3 to
32 characters (`shared/handles.ts`, the same rule as the
`user_handles_handle_check` CHECK and `set_user_handle`). The 'Sharkname'
field of the 'Your name' panel on the Profile's Overview (`SharknameCard`,
beside its 'Friends see' and 'Leaderboards show' choices) shows it, rolls a
new one with Generate (`shared/sharkname.ts`: a camelCase shark name such as
`thirstySharkie`), lets the learner edit it, and saves through
`op=friends-handle`; a taken name answers 409 `handle_taken` and the card
offers another roll. A handle changes at most once every 30 days. Every
handle and every positive guest Challenge run holds one global ordinal from a
transactional counter (`supabase/supabase-schema-063.sql`): a generated name
is the camelCase base plus that number, the number survives a rename and is
never reused, the API sends it as a string, and a preview roll allocates
nothing. The Friends tab says what friends add you by and links to the card.
Remove asks first ("Remove <name>?"), because it ends the friendship on both
sides and only a new request undoes it.

What friends see is two switches on `user_handles`, both off by default, set
through `op=identity` (GET/PUT, signed in, PUT on the per-account write
limit): `show_real_name` (friends see the Google name instead of the
sharkname, offered only to an account with one) and `show_photo_to_friends`
(friends see the Google photo instead of an initials avatar,
`ui/InitialsAvatar`). Switching either on copies the verified Google name and
photo into `user_stats`, where the routines read them.
`friend_display_name()` is the name rule: `friend_list` returns it and the
photo only when switched on; an incoming request carries the asker's chosen
name; a lookup carries it only for an accepted friend, and everyone else sees
the sharkname.

## Live rooms (Play)

`api/play/[action].ts` keeps the answer key on the server. A viewer who may
not see it (every player while a room runs, a multiplayer host included, and
anyone reading a lobby) gets only the questions already shown: none in the
lobby, up to the current one while the room runs. A classroom presenter holds
the whole round with the key, and everyone sees it once the room is finished.
`create` and `join` name the host and each player on the server
(`roomDisplayName` in `lib/public-identity.ts`): the account's Google name
from its verified identity, else its sharkname, else "Player" and four digits
taken from the account id, the same in every room. The server ignores a name
in the request body, and the client sends none, so no part of an email
address reaches a room (`npm run test:launch`, play-rooms suite).
`create` refuses a topic list with an entry that is not a topic id, and
`answer` an option index past the question's options, both with 400; a
room-code clash is left to the unique key on `matches.code`, and `create`
draws another code. A Supabase call that times out answers 503
`upstream_timeout`, and `state`, `answer`, `distribution` and `heartbeat`
answer 503 `backend_unavailable` when they cannot read the room, not 404.
`join` and `state` also send `question_count` and `server_now`. The screens
count each question down against the server's clock, not the device's; the
presenter's key appears once the server stops taking answers, the question's
limit plus a 2 s grace, or when the teacher reveals it.

A classroom question closes when its clock runs out or when the teacher
presses "Reveal answer" (migration 056).
The button is a `control` action, `reveal`: the server stores the question in
`matches.revealed_idx` and `answer` then refuses it with 409
`question_closed` (a retry of an answer that landed before the reveal still
replays it). A classroom answer is only acknowledged (`{ ok, accepted,
advanced: false }`), its retry included: no `is_correct` or speed bonus comes
back, so a pupil learns whether they scored from the scoreboard once the
question closes, and nobody can read the right option off their own reply
while it is open. A multiplayer answer still comes back graded. The
presenter's key shows only after the server confirms, and the pupils' screens
show the question as closed. While a classroom room runs, its
scoreboard counts only closed questions: `state` passes `match_scoreboard`
`p_before_idx`, the current index, or the next one once the current question
is closed. The presenter's projected scoreboard and every pupil's therefore
move only when a question closes, and a live count never tells the room which
option scores. A finished room, and every multiplayer room, counts every
answer. A multiplayer scoreboard shows that a player scored on the
open question, never what they picked, and a multiplayer race tells each
player their own result at once by design.

A class shares one school address, and each state read spends that address's
bucket. Realtime therefore carries only changes of the room: `match_updated`
with `{ status, current_index }` on start, next, finish and the answer that
moves a multiplayer room on, plus `revealed_idx` when the teacher closes a
classroom question, and a screen that already shows that state reads
nothing. A single multiplayer answer sends `answered`, which only the host
handles, with one read at most every 1.5 s for the live scoreboard; a
classroom answer sends nothing, since its scoreboard waits for the question
to close. A read can move a
multiplayer room on by itself (an expired question, or the last answers of an
untimed round racing); the host announces a change their read found, and a
read that loses that race gets the room as the winner left it. Each screen
also reads once when a timed question expires (a multiplayer room moves on,
a classroom scoreboard counts the closed question), and every 30 s as
a healing poll, or every 4 s while Realtime is down.

## Coding section

The `/coding` section, the coding phase inside Learn levels and the GitHub
garden share one server-owned grader.

### Catalogue

The section lists four tracks: `javascript`, `typescript`, `react` and
`algorithms`. The first three are also Learn topics. `algorithms` holds sixty
interview problems in plain JavaScript and two short paths, all graded and
paid like any other task, plus the 29 DSA Foundations tasks (`alg-dsa-*`,
topic `dsa`) and the 15 DSA Advanced tasks (`alg-dsaa-*`, topic
`dsa-advanced`). `levelCodingTasks` seals a DSA task into its Learn level
(DSA Foundations level 15, the review, has none), and `hasLearnLevel` names a
level for the JavaScript, TypeScript and React tracks and for a `dsa` or
`dsa-advanced` task; the row, the workbench and the GitHub commit message
show it (for example "DSA · Level n"). The other
algorithms tasks carry `algorithms` as their topic and never join a Learn
level's quota; their `level` is an ordering key.

`shared/coding-index.ts` lists 769 issued tasks: 480 Easy, 189 Medium and 100
Hard; by track, 316 JavaScript, 156 TypeScript, 183 React and 114 Algorithms.
144 of them are free ("Tiers and billing"). The full catalogue holds 814: the
other 45 are the system-design track, which stays hidden pending an owner
decision (#391; the review and fixes are in
`docs/audit/system-design-review-2026-10-22.md`). `lib/coding/active.ts`
issues no task of a track missing from `CODING_SECTION_TRACKS`, so no
system-design task is in a list, a search, a Challenge run, a skip
suggestion, a Learn level, the browser index or a share page; the task
resource answers 404 for one, Submit and the reveal refuse a sealed session
for one, and a star saved on one is not listed. In the browser
`/coding/system-design` and its task addresses are the Coding not-found page.
The tasks, their sealed-key graders and the `DesignRunner` stay in the
repository, and `npm run test:coding` keeps proving them.

Tiers open in order (`tierUnlocked`): 1 and 2 at once; 3 after every task of
tiers 1 and 2 of that track, and JavaScript's 3 also after the ten JavaScript
Learn levels; 4 after 80 % of tier 3; 5 (React only) after 80 % of tier 4. The
task page, a practice run, a skip's suggestion and the browser all ask that
one rule. `algorithms` and system design are unladdered: an interview
candidate needs the exact problem they came for. Each task also carries Easy,
Medium or Hard (`difficultyOf`), projected from its tier, or for a project
milestone or path level from its position; a checkpoint reads its tier like a
standalone task, so every checkpoint is Easy. The label gates and pays
nothing (see `docs/interactive-content-manifest.md`).

### Grading

The server owns grading. JavaScript and TypeScript submissions run in a
QuickJS WebAssembly sandbox on a worker thread of `api/quiz/roadmap.ts`
(`resource=coding-submit`) with a 2.5 s deadline, which the host enforces by
stopping the thread 1.5 s past it, and virtual timers. Hidden checks run in a
fresh program in a per-submission shuffled order, and a hidden run's error
comes back only as a fixed message (a timeout, a stack overflow, a call that
never settled), never as text the program wrote. Checks that count how code
touches its input use a `Proxy` and helpers taken before the learner's code
runs (`__probe`, `shared/coding-evaluate.ts`), so replacing the global `Proxy`
changes no count. TypeScript type tests run through the real compiler, each
in its own file, on a worker thread of their own
(`lib/coding/ts-check-pool.ts`) that is stopped 4 s into the check; the
learner sees a timeout whose message names the type check. Submit sends the
code at once, and the browser's own run (its type check has 8 s once the
compiler is loaded) is a preview the server's run replaces. The same thread
transpiles the TypeScript, so code nested too deeply for the compiler is an
`error` verdict, not an HTTP 500. React code that nests too deeply for its
compiler fails the same way, before any sandbox starts. System-design answers
are graded against a key sealed in the coding session.

A coding session seals the exact task and grader version (hidden checks,
puzzle material and efficiency guidance). Submit refuses a stale or
unversioned session before grading or writing progress or XP. Reading the
latest brief renews a linked session under the same Learn parent and attempt
id, and the editor keeps its draft. The version stays inside the encrypted
token. A runtime-only grading or reward change must bump the attempt tag in
`lib/coding/session.ts`.

**Capacity and rate limits.** Both thread pools bound how long a run waits
for a thread, and their queues hold a class of 32 submitting at once twice
over. One caller (an account, or an address for a guest) has at most two
submits grading at once on one task or learning-path activity, and three
across all of them, one fewer than an instance's four QuickJS threads
(`GRADING_PER_TASK`, `GRADING_PER_CALLER` in `lib/coding/grader-capacity.ts`,
`enterInFlight` in `lib/rate-limit.ts`). A Submit the grader cannot take is
answered `grader_busy` with a Retry-After, 429 for the caller's own limit and
503 for full threads, and is recorded nowhere. Submit is limited per task
(`enforcePerItemRateLimit`): 30 in ten minutes for one caller on one task
(`codingSubmit`; for a learning-path activity, `learningPathSubmit`), beneath
120 in ten minutes across every task (`codingSubmitCeiling`,
`learningPathSubmitCeiling`). The address backstop holds a class of 32 at the
per-task rate (960 in ten minutes). A spent task answers 429 `rate_limited`
("Too many requests. Wait a moment and try again."); one token comes back
every 20 seconds, and another task stays open. A thread that will not start
is tried again after a pause that grows from one second to thirty; on a
deployment no run falls back to the request thread. Learning-path code
activities use the same sandbox, type-check thread and hidden-check program.

**One realm for Run and Submit.** Local time is UTC, with no summer time,
whatever the server's or the learner's zone: the local getters read what the
UTC getters read, `getTimezoneOffset()` is 0, `toString` ends in "GMT+0000
(Coordinated Universal Time)", and `toLocale*String` formats in UTC unless the
code names a zone. One prelude (`shared/coding-utc-time.ts`) rewrites `Date`'s
local forms in QuickJS, in the Run worker, in the React frame and in the
React guest's page realm (the guest also runs with `TZ=UTC`). Where `Intl`
exists (the two React runners) a named zone formats as `Intl` does; where it
does not (QuickJS, and Run, which hides `Intl` to match), a named UTC or
Europe/Prague zone formats and any other is refused with a message. No task's
check depends on the zone: `test:coding` runs every board of a task that uses
calendar dates in four other zones. `toLocaleString`, `localeCompare` and
their kin print and sort as an en-US browser does (`shared/coding-locale.ts`).
The grader adds `URL`, `URLSearchParams`, `TextEncoder`, `TextDecoder`, `atob`
and `btoa` (`lib/coding/sandbox-web-apis.ts`); the host's WHATWG parser reads
`URL` through a function that takes and returns strings only. Just before
learner code runs, the Run worker removes every global and built-in member
the grader lacks (`shared/coding-checker-globals.ts`: `Intl`, `crypto`,
`fetch` and some two hundred more), and using one reads "`Intl` isn't
available in the checker. Submit runs without it." React tasks keep the full
browser and Node built-ins in both runners. Task-specific built-in
restrictions live in `shared/coding-task-runtime.ts`; the server derives them
from the canonical task id, and Learn reuses the same policy. Clone
Promise.all, for one, replaces the native `Promise.all` with a
non-replaceable throwing function before learner code initializes, in the
grader and in the Run worker.

**Complexity.** A pass pays 25 XP (`CODING_TASK_XP`), or 15
(`CODING_ABOVE_TARGET_XP`) when the analysis reliably identifies a solution
above the task's authored time-complexity target. Unknown complexity pays the
base and shows no complexity row. The analysis withholds an estimate for
returned closures, callable aliases, methods and accessors, callable
containers, input-dependent templates and string additions whose output work
it cannot resolve. One analysis spends at most 150,000 node visits outside
the cost walk (`ANALYSIS_STEPS` in `lib/coding/complexity.ts`), shared by
every call of a helper; past that it reports no estimate ("The analysis limit
was reached"). Dynamic value flow still needs manual review; the check is
bounded, not a general type analysis.

**System design** returns, for a failed or partly right submission, only
whether each step was right and the learner's own answer (`gradeDesign`). The
correct options, orders and ranges, the explanations and the reference answer
travel only with a pass.

**React** submissions use `lib/coding/react-isolated.ts`: a fresh Vercel
Sandbox microVM with network denied, no application credentials, a 256 MB
Node heap and an externally enforced command deadline.
`REACT_RUNNER_SNAPSHOT_ID` selects the dependency-only snapshot; each run
uploads the current grader bundle. `lib/coding/react-runner.ts` is trusted
test and guest code only and must never evaluate learner code inside an API
process. Coding and learning-path React submissions both use this boundary.
A React task can keep hidden cases beside its solution; Submit runs them after
the visible suite and returns only their count. A case during which a form
submits without `preventDefault()` fails, on Submit and on Run, because in the
preview that submission reloads the frame. See
[`react-grading-operations.md`](./react-grading-operations.md). The
self-hosted `client/sandbox/` iframe serves the preview and the Run button's
immediate feedback; the server's verdict is the record.

Every issued task is `verify: 'tests'`. A `verify: 'checklist'` pass would be
recorded as unverified, with no XP, no coins, no link to a Learn level attempt
and no junior or senior solutions; an old unverified pass does not block the
first verified pass's XP.

### Solutions, reveals and XP

Reference solutions never leave the server. `resource=coding-reveal` returns
one only after a pass or after the authored hint ladder is exhausted, and a
reveal ends the current Learn level attempt; inside the level the workbench
stays on screen with the solution open. A reveal before the task's first pass
costs the task its XP and coins: the next pass reports `xpForfeited`, the
reveal confirmation warns a signed-in learner who has not passed, and the
pass shows "Passed +0 XP". After a pass the solution opens without the
warning.

After a pass the learner sees junior and senior readings side by side,
stripped of their authoring comments in `lib/coding/solutions/index.ts`; the
content contract executes the stripped text and asserts it holds no comment.
The contract also holds statements to their promises: when a statement
promises a new array, a version of the reference that mutates and returns
its input must fail a check; and when a TypeScript milestone's parameters
are typed as anything but `unknown`, its type tests must fail the reference
with every parameter typed `any`.

XP follows `CODING_TASK_XP` on a task's first verified pass, once per account
and task, with its coins. It pays again ("repeat XP") on a verified pass after
the learner reset the task to its starter (Reset calls
`?resource=coding-reset`, recorded by `record_coding_reset`), at least 60
minutes after the task last paid or a reveal forfeited it, with no reveal
since the reset (`record_coding_repeat_reveal`). Any verified pass closes the
reset, so each payment takes a reset of its own. A repeat is XP only, under
its own award id (`coding:<account>:<task>:r<n>`), into the balance and the
month's ledger: no coins. The verdict's `repeatXp` says when the XP opens
again and why a pass paid nothing; the workbench says it under the verdict,
and a toast says "+N XP earned", "+N XP again" or what earns the XP again.
Guests earn no XP, so a guest's Reset stays in the browser. The five coding
badges join the shared badge sync. Storage starts in
`supabase/supabase-schema-025.sql`; `record_coding_verdict` keeps the
type-only, unverified, reveal, cooldown and repeat rules.

devShark ships no AI feature: the last hint rung is a documentation link from
`shared/coding-docs.ts`. The skeleton rung before it is written for its task:
`npm run test:coding` fills the skeletons listed in
`scripts/fixtures/skeleton-fills.ts` and requires the filling to keep every
skeleton line and pass every check, and it requires each unfilled skeleton to
fail them.

### Coding inside Learn levels

Learn levels of the `LEARN_CODING_TOPICS` (`javascript`, `typescript`,
`react`, `dsa` and `dsa-advanced`) carry one to three coding tasks sealed into the level session; for an account,
completion requires a verified, unrevealed pass of each. Learn creates the
parent attempt, with its sealed task list, before it serves the intro or a
coding link. A `Build it` link opens a new tab with the coding session in the
URL fragment; that tab removes the fragment and submits the session in the
request body. The server checks the owner, task binding, expiry and open
attempt on access, Submit and reveal. A read-only attempt-status request
checks completion when Learn regains focus. Missing coding passes leave the
attempt open, recording no failure, XP or streak; a reveal still ends it. The
coding routines check the attempt again under its row lock, so a verdict or a
reveal for an attempt that ended mid-request (completed in another tab, or
past its two hours) answers 409 `invalid_session` and records nothing, as
does completing an expired attempt.
`supabase/supabase-schema-062.sql` enforces the same rule under the parent
row lock. A coding task issued inside a Learn level follows that level's
access tier.

A guest's coding passes are never stored, so a guest's level passes on its
questions: the completion answers `codingUnverified: true`, records nothing,
and the browser keeps the pass on that device and says coding tasks are
checked and saved after sign-in. The Easy, Medium and Hard bands of the
standalone catalogue (`EASY_BAND`, `MEDIUM_HARD_BAND` in
`lib/coding/catalog.ts`) never join a level's quota, except the band tasks
mapped into DSA Advanced levels.

Coding completion is permanent: the API returns an empty due queue and never
schedules a passed task for review. The browser shows no coding review: no
"Due for review" filter, no coding card on Today, and `/coding/review`
redirects to `/coding`. Question and concept review is separate.

### Drafts

A task's code is kept on the device about a second after the learner stops
typing (leaving the page writes what is waiting), and on Run and Submit;
code over 20 kB is kept there too. Signed in, Run and Submit also save it as
the account draft (`?op=coding-draft`, 20 kB at most), never typing. A task in
the section and a Learn level's coding step follow the same rules
(`client/src/coding/drafts.ts`). The device copy records the account draft's
`updated_at` it builds on (the task response carries `draftUpdatedAt`). When
the copies differ the page says which one is open, and a device copy that did
not open is set aside and offered back ("Open the code from this device")
until the learner takes it or the account confirms a save. A save sends that
time as `base`, and `save_coding_draft_v2` writes only while the account draft
still has it: otherwise the server answers 409 `draft_conflict` with the
stored time (never its code), and the page asks whether to save this code
over it or open the other draft, keeping this device's code either way. When
other saves keep creating the same first draft beside it, the routine gives
up after three tries, writes nothing, and the server answers 503 `draft_busy`
with Retry-After. A draft save, or a signed-in evolving stage's Submit, whose
code holds U+0000 (which Postgres text cannot store) answers 400. A task whose
account draft cannot be read answers 500 instead of opening as if it had
none. An evolving stage's Submit writes the stage draft through the same
routine, forced, and returns its time.

A sign-out the learner chooses (Log out, here or in another tab, or deleting
the account) forgets the drafts with the rest of the account's data. A
session that ends on its own (an expired or revoked refresh, a password
changed elsewhere) keeps the drafts under the account's id and out of sight,
says "You were signed out — sign in to keep your code", and gives them back
when the same account signs in and opens a coding task. Another account
signing in deletes them unread (`client/src/lib/accountData.ts`). A task open
at the sign-out reads itself again, so the account's code leaves the editor.
Code typed a moment before and still waiting is written first
(`beforeAccountChange`), and work begun for the account that left (a draft
read, a brief refresh) checks `accountEpoch` and opens nothing.

### What a task screen loads, and when

The workbench's heavy parts load when the learner reaches for them, not when
a page opens (perf audit #587). None of this changes what is graded, saved or
shown once it has loaded.

- **Prettier** (about 170 KB gzip) loads on the first Format press. Nothing
  formats in the background: Format is on whenever the code differs from the
  starter as authored or from what the last press produced, and a press that
  changes nothing turns it off until the next edit. Code Prettier cannot read
  says why under the editor and turns Format off until the next edit. Before,
  the page formatted the whole program 350 ms after it opened and after every
  typing pause to decide whether Format was on (C02, C24).
- **A hidden track** (system design) is the not-found page before anything
  loads: the track page and its task addresses read no plan, progress, stars
  or run and warm no workbench (C26).

### Evolving projects and short paths

`shared/evolving.ts` holds fourteen projects with 146 stages and fifteen
short paths with 75 levels. A single-track project has ten stages and a
full-stack project twelve. `-start` checkpoints separate setup, data loading
and form wiring from the behaviour after them, each with a hint ladder of its

are cumulative, and each stage lists its own checks first so Results opens on
what the brief just asked for. A passed milestone also covers the checkpoint
before it, without an extra XP receipt. The registry controls routes, unlocks
and progress.

Ten projects are on the Coding home and the three full-stack apps on the
FullStack screen. The ten-stage debugging project is `unlisted`: no list shows
it, and its task ids still open, grade and keep their drafts and passes.
Twelve short paths sit on their section's page (five JavaScript, two each for
TypeScript, React and Algorithms, and a link shortener that leads the

Every graded task carries a reference, a junior and a senior solution on the
server; the last two reach the browser only with a verified pass. A stage's
solutions hold only what that stage asks for: the content contract grades
them against the next stage and its checkpoint (the reference alone for React
stages) and fails when one passes. A checkpoint carries its own reference,
the previous stage's code plus the checkpoint's one step, and no junior or
senior board; the contract fails when that reference passes the milestone it
leads to. The three checkpoints whose honest reference already does the
milestone's work are listed in `SAME_AS_MILESTONE`
(`scripts/test-coding-content.ts`), and the contract lets one be free only
when its milestone is.

### GitHub garden

The GitHub garden is an optional GitHub App integration (`lib/github-app.ts`,
`lib/github-handlers.ts`): the learner installs the app on one repository
they own, and every passed task is committed there as one file per task in
one folder per track (`gardenPathFor`). The app requests user authorization
during installation, so GitHub returns the learner to `/settings/github` with
a one-time `code` beside `installation_id` and the sealed `state`.
`github-connect-finish` checks the state (the devShark account that started),
exchanges the code for a user access token, and accepts the installation only
when it appears in that user's `GET /user/installations` (every page) and its
account is the same GitHub user (`GET /user`), which refuses a collaborator
who can see the owner's installation. The user token serves those two reads
and is dropped. A missing or refused code, an organisation installation or an
installation that is not the user's own saves nothing, and the callback page
asks the learner to install and authorize again. GitHub's code works once, so
the callback offers Retry only after a failure that never reached the check
(no connection, 429 or 503); after any other failure it offers Connect again
(`github-connect-start`).

Connect and sync are limited per learner (`githubConnect`, `githubSync`, keyed
`user:<id>`) behind a class-sized address backstop (`githubConnectAddress`,
`githubSyncAddress`). A unique index on `github_connections.installation_id`
keeps one installation to one devShark account; a second account gets 409
`installation_taken`. No user token is stored; the server mints installation
tokens from the app key on demand, queues failed commits for a later sync,
and deletes the connection and the queue on disconnect. The feature stays
hidden, and `github-connection` answers `available: false`, until
`GITHUB_APP_ID`, `GITHUB_APP_SLUG`, `GITHUB_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`,
`GITHUB_APP_CLIENT_ID` and `GITHUB_APP_CLIENT_SECRET` all exist on the
deployment (`isGithubAppConfigured`); until then the Profile shows no garden
card. `npm run test:garden` runs the connect step against a stand-in GitHub.

## Learning paths

One optional path sits on top of the Learn levels and the Coding section and
replaces neither. **Forward Deployed Engineer** is a *role specialization*:
the learner picks Fullstack, Frontend or Backend first, then adds FDE on top.
It is the only path (`LEARNING_PATH_IDS` is `['fde']`) and occupies the single
`specialization` slot of the account preference. `docs/fde-curriculum.md` is
its module table.

**Availability.** `LEARNING_PATH_FDE_ENABLED=true` switches the path on; a
path opens only when its switch is on, its content validates and migration
026 is installed. Production does not set the variable today, so the path is
previewable and reports `disabled`. Switching it on is an owner item in
`NEEDED.md`.

### What the path holds

- **The guided loop.** Fourteen teaching modules, M01 Customer discovery to
  M14 Codify the pattern, in five delivery phases, then the staged capstone
  C01 (the support operations workbench: four graded code stages and four
  self-reviewed written pieces). The capstone's `dependsOn` names M01–M14
  explicitly, and `scripts/learning-path-schema-contracts.ts` fails when that
  list omits or reorders a teaching module or names a shelf module. Activity
  ids keep the slugs they were authored under, so M03's items start
  `fde-v1-m02-` and M02's `fde-v1-m02-cases-`. The path header counts 15
  modules, 28 lessons, 120 checks, 15 coding exercises, 10 written artifacts
  and 40–60 hours (`pathInventory`, from the manifest the API sends).
- **Off the loop.** The diagnostic ("Where you already are", twelve scenario
  questions and two debugging exercises) reports per competency and never
  gates. The optional bridges (backend mapping, operator state, an incident
  case, and Python reading) are recommendations. The interview-practice shelf
  IP01–IP04 (`shelf: 'interview-prep'`) holds two code rounds of three
  exercises each, with 300–500-line fixtures, server-only references and
  hidden assertions; twelve design cases graded as objective checks; five
  self-reviewed reflections; and eight lessons with primary references. The
  shelf never supplies the next action, counts toward completion, or changes
  the competency and phase counts or the path's time estimate.
- **Any nine of twelve.** M02 Decomposition cases completes with both lessons
  and any nine of its twelve four-question cases at `verified_pass`
  (`requiresAtLeast`).

`shared/learning-paths.ts` owns the vocabulary: `LearningPathId`,
`LearningPathKind`, `RoleSpecializationId`, the evidence states and the pure
completion arithmetic. It stays apart from the other registries: `Track` keeps
its three values, the cosmetic `Specialization` label in `leveling.ts` is a
different concept with a similar name, and neither `CodingTrack` nor
`RoadmapTopic` gains a member. A path *references* those registries and never
joins them.

### Access

Enrolment and resumption are free. The `learning-path-activity` rule in
`shared/tiers.ts` reads the manifest: every activity of the diagnostic module
and of the first required module (M01) is free, and the rest is Premium.
Optional and shelf modules never become the first required module, and an
unknown path or activity fails closed. The server checks the tier at start
and at submit, after verifying the caller owns the enrolment, and answers a
locked activity with 402 `premium_required`. A lapsed account reopens its
recorded verified passes and self-reviewed work; draft reads and saves keep
their access. The client marks each locked activity and the server's next
action and opens the usual upgrade sheet. The picker can save a free FDE
preference when the catalogue is unavailable, which never invents an
enrolment.

Home, the landing and Premium introduce the path from the fetched manifest
through `pathInventory`; a failed or pending catalogue read shows no
invented inventory, and the section stays a preview while the path is
disabled. The shared Premium inclusion list names FDE and states the free
diagnostic and first-module boundary; `shared/tiers.ts` remains the only
statement of what Premium opens.

### Hub and evidence

The FDE overview lives at `/learn/forward-deployed-engineer/path`, with module

### Contract and grading

`shared/learning-path-api.ts` is the wire contract. Authored content, answer
keys, hidden assertions and reference solutions live under
`lib/learning-paths/` and reach the browser only as projections that strip
them. `lib/learning-paths/catalog.ts` never imports the solutions, and the
launch contracts forbid `client/` from importing `lib/learning-paths`.

Endpoints are `resource=`/`op=` branches on existing handlers
(`learning-path-catalog`, `-start` and `-submit` on `api/quiz/roadmap.ts`;
`learning-preference`, `learning-path-enrollment`, `-progress`, `-draft` and
`-reward` on `api/user/[op].ts`). Storage starts in
`supabase/supabase-schema-026.sql`.

Grading reuses the QuickJS sandbox, the TypeScript checker and the 2.5-second
deadline. The path adapter adds an attempt bound to an enrolment (so the
enrolment authorizes a task reached through the path, not the coding tier
ladder), assertions grouped into named criteria (so a security-critical
failure cannot be averaged away), and evidence recorded in `learning_path_*`
only. A path pass writes no tier unlock. A verified code activity records its
acceptance and the shared coding award in one transaction
(`supabase/supabase-schema-065.sql`): a reused ordinary task keeps its task id
and cannot earn a second first-pass reward, and any other path exercise has a
stable scoped id (`lib/learning-paths/coding-task-id.ts`). The accepted
response stores the actual award and the complexity analysis that chose it,
so a retry replays it without paying again. Non-coding activities keep their
own reward rules.

A code session binds the exact activity, grader material and rubric version.
A stale unaccepted session is refused before grading or any evidence or XP
write; loading the latest brief keeps the editor source. An automatic retry
sends its previous seal when it starts a replacement attempt; a changed,
legacy or expired code attempt needs a latest-brief review before another
Submit. An accepted network retry returns the stored response read-only. A
check whose attempt expired says so ("This attempt expired. Try again to
start a new one.") and deals a new attempt only when the learner asks,
because a silent deal would clear their answers. A runtime-only grading
change must bump the attempt tag in `lib/learning-paths/session.ts`.

An objective check grades against the key sealed in its attempt. A project
check (`purpose: 'project'`; none is published) deals the same questions again
on a retry, reshuffled, so after a failed attempt it returns only which
questions were wrong. A diagnostic or exercise check returns the correct
options and explanations after every attempt. A domain-gated check needs its
threshold overall and a minimum in every domain: the same threshold for a
domain of five or more questions, half for a smaller one.

Three optional content fields shape the path. A check's `scenario` is a shared
case the learner reads once above its ordered questions; the check needs three
questions or more, and the scenario ships in the public manifest, so it must
never quote an answer or an explanation. A code activity's `fixtureNote` sits
above the editor and says the fixture is unfamiliar on purpose and what "done"
means. A module's `shelf: 'interview-prep'` lists it apart from the loop and
must come with `optional: true`. The validator refuses a misplaced field
(`misplaced_scenario`, `misplaced_fixture_note`), a short scenario check
(`scenario_too_few_questions`), an empty one (`missing_copy`), an unknown or
non-optional shelf (`unknown_shelf`, `shelf_not_optional`) and a loop module
that depends on a shelf module (`loop_depends_on_shelf`). It warns when a
scenario passes 2,000 characters (`long_scenario`) or a shelf module comes
ahead of a loop module (`shelf_before_loop`). `isOffLoopModule` in
`shared/learning-paths.ts` decides which modules never gate completion, supply
the next action or count toward the module total; `pathInventory` also leaves
a shelf module's work out of the header counts.

### Drafts, acceptance and completion

A code exercise or a written piece autosaves one draft per activity, up to
`PATH_LIMITS.draftsPerEnrollment` (60) per enrolment, which `npm run
test:paths` keeps at or above every path's code exercises plus artifacts. A
pass keeps its draft, the only copy of the learner's passed code the server
holds. `purge_expired_learning_data` removes drafts idle for 90 days. A draft
or artifact holding U+0000 answers 400, and a first save that races another
answers 409 `draft_conflict`.

The browser keeps an unsaved draft on the device too
(`client/src/lib/learningPathWorkspace.ts`), with the server revision it was
typed on; once the server has moved past that revision, the server's copy
wins. Starting an activity returns its draft in the same request, and
`draftUnavailable: true` when the server could not read it: the device copy
then stays for the next save to settle. Switching activity, leaving the view
or a change of account writes what is waiting first (`beforeAccountChange`).
An involuntary sign-out keeps these copies for that account alone, as it does
coding drafts ("Drafts" under "Coding section"); another account signing in
deletes them unread. In a code activity, Reset asks before it replaces
changed code with the starter, and the code editor loads only when a code
activity renders.

`accept_learning_path_result` (migration 054) keeps a verified pass and a
submitted written piece against any weaker later attempt, which is still
stored as evidence. It accepts one result per enrolment at a time and decides
module completion from the module's requirements, so two submits finishing a
module together still complete it. A quota module such as M02 uses
`accept_learning_path_quota_result` (migration 066), which counts all plain
requirements and the nine-of-twelve quota from recorded states under the same
enrolment lock; a precomputed completion flag cannot bypass either condition.
A coding quota activity keeps the atomic coding award, and a replay never
pays twice. Without the quota routine the submit answers 503
`migration_required`.

Completion language is narrow. The server says "FDE guided path completed"
only after every required loop module and every capstone stage is complete.
The ten written pieces in M01, M13, M14 and the capstone are reviewed by the
learner against the rubric (`self_reviewed`, never counted as verified), and
the path needs all of them. The overview lists them on their own line,
"Written pieces you review yourself". There is no certification, no rank and
no claim about employment.

Finishing the path would also earn the merchandise package
(`path_is_complete`, every module completed within the one enrolment of the
published curriculum version). Merchandise is paused ("Merchandise"), so
`learning-path-reward` answers 404 `merch_unavailable` today.

## Practice, scheduling and what the product claims about it

Four shared modules each own one job.

`shared/concepts.ts` names the unit practice is scheduled and ordered by. A
category is a shelf ("javascript" says where a question lives, not what it
teaches), so twenty authored groups resolve fifty concepts from tags the
banks already carry. A concept never spans categories, which stops a mixed
session reaching into a topic the learner has not unlocked.

`shared/spaced-practice.ts` decides *which* concepts are due, and
`shared/interleave.ts` decides *what order* a session is played in. Keeping
them apart keeps both testable and stops the ordering policy turning into a
second scheduler. Both are pure with the clock injected; persistence is
`concept_reviews` (migration 030), written only through an idempotent
service-role routine. Only independent retrieval lengthens an interval: a
hinted, revealed or recognition-shaped answer holds the rung it already
earned. Review grants no XP of its own and never widens the pool it draws
from. When the learner's review states cannot be read, a session still runs
as if none were stored, but the submit records no concept review, since each
next state builds on the one read (`loadReviewStates` answers null). See
`docs/practice-scheduling.md`.

`shared/curation.ts` owns what the product may *say* about its content: the
two gates (relevance out of ten, quality out of five, both required) and the
derivations that fail closed. No record means not reviewed, a record for a
different version means not reviewed, "more than once" needs more than one
recorded human review, and unreadable metadata produces no sentence at all.
`lib/curation.ts` computes the keyed content version that makes "an edit
invalidates the approval" enforceable, and applies the eligibility rule: a
recorded decision in the registry generated from the audit ledger
(`docs/audit/devshark-content-ledger.json` → `lib/curation-registry.ts`)
holds for exactly the content it was made about (a retirement withholds, a
current passing review serves), and in scope an item with no applicable
record is withheld too. The ledger's scope covers every served question
category and the coding catalogue (`codingTasks: true`), so an unrecorded
question or coding task is withheld rather than served. The rule runs once,
in the questions store and the active coding catalogue, so every selector
sees the same set. See `docs/curation-claims.md`.

`shared/lesson-figures.ts` holds the authored worked examples shown in Learn
level intros: deterministic HTML and CSS, never generated imagery, each
carrying its content in words rather than a caption. See
`docs/lesson-figures.md`.

## Tiers and billing

devShark is freemium. Every registered account gets the free tier. Premium
costs 4.99 EUR a month or 59.88 EUR a year before VAT (`PREMIUM_PRICE` in
`shared/tiers.ts`); Checkout adds applicable VAT. Premium changes which
content a learner may start. Grading, explanations, XP amounts, scores,
streaks, ranks, leaderboards and matchmaking work the same on both tiers.

Billing is built and off: the owner keeps sales disabled (#221;
`STRIPE_INTEGRATION_TODO.md` tracks the Stripe setup). Until sales open,
Premium opens through a manual grant or a voucher.

### The free tier

- **`shared/tiers.ts`** is the one contract for what free includes: HTML, CSS
  and JavaScript in full (`FREE_LEARN_TOPICS`), React levels 1 to 12 of 25,
  and DSA Foundations levels 1 to 5 with their part test
  (`FREE_LEARN_LEVELS`; a part test is Premium when any level of its part
  is). The FDE path's diagnostic and first required module are free ("Learning
  paths"). Also free are the opening stages of every evolving project and
  short path (`FREE_EVOLVING_STAGES`, `freeStageCount`: stage one, which in a
  project with checkpoints is the checkpoint and the first milestone, plus
  the checkpoint that opens the next milestone; stages 1, 2 and 3 of a
  ten-stage project, 1 and 2 of a twelve-stage full-stack app, and level 1 of
  a short path) and a starter set of standalone coding challenges listed once
  in `FREE_CODING_TASK_IDS`. `npm run build:coding-index` projects all of it
  into `shared/coding-index.ts` as `free: true`: 144 of the 769 issued tasks
  (JavaScript 68, TypeScript 32, React 30, Algorithms 14), held between 16 and
  22 % by the launch contracts (`FREE_CODING_SHARE`). Every free task reads
  Easy except two debugging paths whose authored level 1 is Medium. Re-pick
  the list, not the task files, when the catalogue grows; the launch
  contracts walk every project as a free account to prove each free stage
  opens after free passes only.
- Quizzes, the daily challenge, the Biggest Shark Challenge, multiplayer,
  flashcards, the typing racer, leaderboards, streaks, friends and the coin
  shop stay open: no quiz category is gated, so nothing in this module names
  one.
- `contentTier` and `isOpenTo` take a `GatedContent` value and pure index
  data. The module imports nothing from `lib/`, so the browser draws its locks
  and the server refuses with the same function, and unknown content is
  Premium. A signed-out visitor holds the free tier, unsaved: the landing
  sample question ("Try one, no signup") runs in the browser, and every
  Premium level, part test, challenge and stage is refused to a guest with the
  same 402 as to a free account.

### Enforcement

- **`lib/access.ts`** resolves the tier once per request (`resolveTier`, one
  `is_premium` call, only when the content is Premium), and `assertOpen`
  throws `PremiumRequiredError` for locked content; `refuseLocked` is the
  handler form. A tier the database cannot read answers 503
  `entitlement_unavailable`, and so does a cleared-content read that fails
  (the `cleared` callback throws), so a lapsed learner is never shown the
  upgrade sheet for work they passed. The call sites are the Learn level, part-test and
  checkpoint seals and their answers, `coding-task`, `coding-submit`,
  `coding-reveal`, evolving stage two and up, `learning-path-start` and
  `learning-path-submit` in `api/quiz/roadmap.ts`, and
  `learning-path-enrollment` in `api/user/[op].ts`. Premium gates starting new
  content. Drafts, bookmarks, skips and reviews of cleared content stay open;
  a new coding bookmark or collection item needs the same access as opening
  the task, and an old bookmark stays removable. A lapsed account keeps
  everything it passed, and placement checks stay open. A coding task issued
  inside a Learn level follows that level, and pausing a learning path is
  never refused.
- **The 402 contract.** `jsonPremiumRequired` in `lib/http.ts` (and
  `withRequestContext` for anything thrown) answers `PremiumRequiredError`
  with HTTP 402 in the standard error envelope:
  `{ error: { code: 'premium_required', message, kind, ref, requestId } }`.
  The API client in `client/src/lib/api.ts` turns that response into the
  upgrade sheet, so a stale client never shows a raw error. `op=entitlement`
  (GET, signed in) returns `{ tier, source, currentPeriodEnd,
  cancelAtPeriodEnd, inGrace, validUntil, billingAccount, subscriptionLive }`
  from `entitlement_summary`; the last two say whether the account has a
  Stripe customer and a subscription Stripe can still charge (`active`,
  `trialing`, `past_due`, `unpaid` or `paused`), whichever grant wins the plan
  line.
- **The browser** mirrors the server. `useEntitlement()`
  (`client/src/lib/entitlement.ts`) reads the plan; `useLocks()`
  (`client/src/lib/locks.ts`) draws locks from `contentTier` in four states:
  open, locked (a signed-in free account), preview (a signed-out visitor on
  Premium content, refused like locked) and unknown (plan loading or offline,
  drawn as open so the server decides). `isBarred` is true for locked and
  preview, and every screen asks it: a barred level, part test, coding row or
  evolving stage stays focusable with `aria-disabled`, reads "Premium" in
  text and opens the one `UpgradeSheet`, which leads a guest to sign-in and
  checkout. An unknown row carries no Premium mark, and the coding track page
  waits for the plan and says when it could not load. Today leaves barred
  levels out, and the Profile shows the plan line.

### Grants and vouchers

- **The billing tables** (`supabase/supabase-schema-039.sql` and later):
  `billing_customers` (one Stripe customer per user), `entitlement_grants`
  (provider, manual and promo grants; the webhook never touches a manual or
  promo row), `billing_events` (provider event ids, so a repeated delivery
  changes nothing), `billing_checkout_consents` and `billing_cancel_requests`.
  `is_premium(p_user)` is true for an active, unexpired manual or promo grant,
  an `active` or `trialing` provider grant, or a `past_due` one within seven
  days of the failed renewal (`past_due_since`, set from the unpaid period's
  start and never moved forward). RLS is on for all of them, with an
  owner-scoped SELECT policy on every table but `billing_events` and
  `billing_cancel_requests`, which have none. The routines (`is_premium`,
  `entitlement_summary`, `link_billing_customer`,
  `upsert_provider_entitlement`, `grant_manual_entitlement`,
  `revoke_manual_entitlement`, `record_billing_event`, `finish_billing_event`,
  `release_billing_event`, `billing_account`, `billing_customer_owner`,
  `record_checkout_consent`, `claim_voluntary_refund` and the cancel-request
  routines) are SECURITY DEFINER with an empty `search_path`, executable by
  `service_role` only, and none reads or writes XP, stats, streak or progress
  tables. `billing_cancel_requests` holds the SHA-256 of each emailed link,
  the address typed on the page, the action and the times, never a token or
  an account id, and each new request purges rows a day past their expiry. A
  revoked provider grant stays revoked, and an account holds at most one
  active manual grant. `link_billing_customer` and
  `upsert_provider_entitlement` refuse an id with no `auth.users` row
  (`unknown_account`). `supabase/tests/160` to `165` run these routines on a
  real Postgres. Admins test Premium with manual grants through
  `op=entitlements` in `api/admin/[op].ts` (GET lists grants, or one
  account's with `?userId=`; POST `{ action: 'grant', userId, validUntil,
  note }` or `{ action: 'revoke', userId, grantId?, note? }`).
- **Premium vouchers (migration 045).** Premium has three sources, each a row
  of `entitlement_grants`: `provider` (a Stripe subscription), `manual` (an
  admin's grant) and `promo`, which a voucher opens. While billing is off, a
  voucher is how a learner gets Premium.
  - *Creating.* The owner works in `/dev` → Vouchers (`op=vouchers` in
    `api/admin/[op].ts`, behind `requireAdmin`): a note, Premium for a number
    of days counted from the redemption or with no end, how many accounts may
    redeem it (one by default), an optional last day, and an optional custom
    code of 6 to 32 letters and digits. A generated code is twelve characters
    of Crockford base32 from `crypto.randomBytes`, shown once as
    `XXXX-XXXX-XXXX`; GET lists vouchers and POST `{ action: 'revoke',
    voucherId }` stops one.
  - *Storing.* `premium_vouchers` keeps the SHA-256 of the normalised code
    (upper case, no spaces or dashes, `shared/vouchers.ts`) and its first four
    characters, never the code. `premium_voucher_redemptions` keeps the
    account, the voucher, the grant and the time, one row per voucher and
    account. Both have RLS on, no policy and no browser grant; the routines
    (`create_premium_voucher`, `list_premium_vouchers`,
    `revoke_premium_voucher`, `redeem_premium_voucher` and the private
    `premium_voucher_json`) are SECURITY DEFINER with an empty `search_path`
    and `service_role` only. No log line carries a code, its hash or its hint.
  - *Redeeming.* A signed-in learner types the code on `/premium` (`op=voucher`
    POST in `api/user/[op].ts`). Five attempts an hour per account and ten
    per address count every try, a success included. An unknown, expired,
    used-up, revoked or malformed code gets one answer, 400
    `voucher_invalid`; only 409 `voucher_already_redeemed` is told apart.
    `redeem_premium_voucher` locks the voucher row, so the last use goes to
    one account, and writes a `promo` grant noted "Voucher <hint>" with
    `valid_until` = now + the days, or none. The browser refetches the plan,
    so the locks open without a reload.
  - *Limits.* A voucher's days start when it is redeemed; two vouchers do not
    add up, and the plan line shows the grant that lasts longest. Revoking a
    voucher stops new redemptions: Premium it already opened stays until its
    end, and an admin ends one grant with `op=entitlements`.
  - *Screens.* While billing is off `/premium` leads with "Have a voucher?"
    (after "Your plan" for an account that holds Premium), the upgrade sheet
    offers "Redeem a voucher", and the Profile's plan line reads "Premium from
    a voucher, until …". With billing on, the voucher follows the plans.
  - *Campaign links.* `/premium?voucher=<code>` fills the field, lands on the
    section and takes the parameter out of the address bar at once, so the
    code stays out of the history, copied links and analytics. It redeems
    nothing by itself. A visitor who has to sign in first keeps the code in
    the tab's session storage for the way back. A campaign voucher's cap and
    last day are `max_redemptions` and `redeemable_until`.

### Stripe

Stripe Checkout, Billing and the Customer Portal take the payments, with
Managed Payments making Stripe (as Link) the merchant of record; plain Stripe
with Stripe Tax runs behind the same code when `STRIPE_MANAGED_PAYMENTS` is
`false`. Only the server talks to Stripe, through the official `stripe`
package (API version `2026-09-30.endive;
custom_checkout_payment_form_preview=v1`). Stripe.js mounts a Stripe-hosted
iframe on `/premium/checkout`, so card details go straight to Stripe; Managed
Payments uses `ui_mode: 'embedded_page'` and plain Stripe `form`. The buyer
consents on devShark before a session exists, and the server records the
consent's version, time and waiver text in session metadata. The CSP permits
the specific Stripe script, frame and connection origins. The code lives in
`lib/billing/` (`config.ts`, `stripe.ts`, `sync.ts`, `cancel.ts`,
`handlers.ts`), and the routes are `op=` branches of `api/user/[op].ts`:

- `billing-checkout` POST `{ plan: 'monthly' | 'annual', consent: true }`
  (signed in, 10 a minute per account): gets or creates the account's Stripe
  customer, sends an account with a subscription that can still charge to the
  portal instead (409 `already_premium`), whether the database or Stripe knows
  it first, expires the customer's open devShark Checkout Sessions, and
  creates a subscription Checkout Session with `client_reference_id`,
  `subscription_data.metadata` `product: 'devshark'` and `supabase_user_id`,
  required terms consent whose text is the withdrawal waiver, the renewal and
  cancellation sentence beside the order button, `submit_type: 'auto'`,
  promotion codes and `locale: 'auto'`. GET `?session_id=` is the success
  page's lookup: only the account's own completed session is applied, through
  the same path as the webhook.
- `billing-portal` POST (signed in, 10 a minute): a Customer Portal session
  that returns to `/profile`.
- `billing-webhook` POST: `Stripe-Signature` over the raw body, 400 when it
  does not verify, no rate limit. It records the event first
  (`record_billing_event`: a processed duplicate answers 200 and stops; a
  delivery that failed part-way is taken again once its 60-second lease
  ends), then refetches the live subscription and upserts. An account the
  server cannot resolve is recorded with its reason and answered 200; any
  other failure is handed back (`release_billing_event`) and answered 500 so
  Stripe retries.
- `billing-cancel` POST, public: `{ email, action: 'cancel' | 'withdraw',
  step: 'request' | 'confirm' }`, then `{ step: 'review' | 'execute', token }`
  from the emailed link. `request` only checks the form. `confirm` (five an
  hour per address) acts at once for a signed-in account whose confirmed email
  is that address; for anyone else it records the request
  (`create_billing_cancel_request`, three links an hour per address) and
  emails a single-use link, valid 60 minutes, to the typed address. That
  answer makes no Stripe call and keeps a 900 ms floor, so it reads the same
  whether or not the address has a subscription; without Resend it asks for
  the sign-in instead. The link opens `/premium/cancel#confirm=<token>`:
  `review` reads what it asks for, and `execute` uses it
  (`consume_billing_cancel_request`) and carries the request out with the
  time it was first made, handing the link back if Stripe cannot be reached.
  It acts on every live devShark subscription of the Stripe customers with
  that email: cancel at period end, or a withdrawal. Within 14 days of the
  account's first payment and once per account (`claim_voluntary_refund`,
  plus a `devshark_voluntary_refund` mark on the Stripe customer), a
  withdrawal refunds every payment of the subscription, ends it at once and
  revokes the grant; otherwise it cancels at period end. Resend emails the
  receipt to the subscription's address.

**Event mapping.** `checkout.session.completed` links the customer and keeps
the consent (`billing_checkout_consents`, one row per session with the text
the buyer saw); subscription created, updated and deleted, `invoice.paid` and
`invoice.payment_failed` mirror the subscription, so `past_due` keeps Premium
for seven days from the failed renewal and the plan line reads "Payment
failed, update your card". Only a subscription that bills a devShark Premium
Price, or an earlier one listed in `STRIPE_PRICE_PREMIUM_LEGACY`, opens
Premium: one that devShark's checkout created and that now bills another
product is written `canceled`, and any other is recorded as
`not_devshark_price`. The `product: 'devshark'` metadata decides first. A full
refund, a dispute, an early fraud warning (which first refunds the charge)
and a withdrawal cancel the subscription at Stripe and write the grant
`revoked` with the reason in its note; a partial refund only logs. Nothing is
cancelled or refunded unless the subscription is devShark's and maps to an
existing account; any other is recorded as `not_devshark_price` or
`unknown_user`. A devShark subscription whose account no longer exists is
cancelled at once, without a final invoice or proration, and logged as
`orphaned_subscription_ended` for the owner to refund by hand. A second
subscription that can charge an account is logged as `second_subscription`;
nothing is refunded automatically. Then `revoke_premium_benefits` (migration
041) takes back what Premium paid out while that grant existed: unsent coin
redemptions are cancelled and their coins returned, and the milestone coins
and the doubled share of XP credits are debited once, as far as the balance
reaches. It does nothing while another grant keeps the account Premium.
`npm run test:billing` proves all of it with signed fixture events
(`scripts/fixtures/billing/`).

**Browser.** `/premium/success` reads the plan and, when the webhook is late,
asks the server to apply the session; after its last automatic check it says
to press Check again. `/premium/cancel` is the public cancellation and
withdrawal page, linked from the footer whenever Stripe is connected; it
takes the token of an emailed link out of the address bar before it asks
anything, and states the real effect of a withdrawal before the last press.
`PremiumCheckoutButton` is the only way into Checkout: with billing off it
reads "Premium opens soon" and shows no button; signed out it signs in and
comes back to the same page (`client/src/lib/authReturn.ts`); an account with

The Profile plan line offers "Manage billing" to every account with a Stripe
customer, on Free as well. Deleting an account ends its subscriptions at
Stripe first, at once and without a refund, and expires its open Checkout
Sessions; it stops if Stripe cannot be reached, and the deletion card says so
to a paying account and links the withdrawal.

**Public copy and legal pages.** `/premium` (`PremiumPage.tsx`) shows the two
plans with "before VAT", the renewal, the waiver sentence and the 14-day
refund "once per account" beside the buttons (`PremiumFacts.tsx`), what
Premium opens, the plan table and six questions; a subscriber sees the plan

twelve monthly payments only when there is one (`annualSaving` in

not-found page answers.

**Launch offer.** `shared/launch-offer.ts` holds a launch price: 55 % off for
the lifetime of every subscription started between 4 October 2026 00:00 and
30 November 2026 23:59:59, Prague time (2.25 EUR a month or 26.95 EUR a year
before VAT, rounded the way Stripe rounds a percentage coupon). The offer is

renders. When on, Checkout (`checkoutSessionParams`) adds `discounts: [{
coupon }]` and drops `allow_promotion_codes`; the coupon is `duration:
forever` with `redeem_by` at the end of the window, and a discounted session
expires when the offer does. The browser shows the offer only when
`/api/settings` says `billing.launchOffer` and its own clock is inside the
window (`client/src/lib/launchOffer.ts`). `/premium`, the plan table, the
upgrade sheet, the expired-checkout line and the Terms then print the launch
price beside the regular price struck through, with "kept for the lifetime of
your subscription · cancel anytime" and "Offer ends 30 Nov 2026"; screen
readers hear one sentence per price, and there is no countdown or scarcity
copy. The pages frame it as an introductory price: Premium was never sold
before, so no page cites a "lowest price in the last 30 days", and the launch
contracts refuse such a line. The static `/premium`, the JSON-LD offers and
the share images state the regular price. `LAUNCH_OFFER_TEST_NOW` replaces
the clock for the coupon decision on a test-mode deployment only (an
`sk_test_` or `rk_test_` key).

**Stripe environment:** `BILLING_ENABLED`, `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`,
`STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `STRIPE_PRICE_PREMIUM_MONTHLY`,
`STRIPE_PRICE_PREMIUM_ANNUAL`, `STRIPE_MANAGED_PAYMENTS` (`true` or `false`,
no default) and `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` (defaults to `https://devshark.app`), plus
`STRIPE_PRICE_PREMIUM_LEGACY` (optional, earlier Premium Prices still billed),
`STRIPE_COUPON_LAUNCH` (optional) and `RESEND_API_KEY` with `RESEND_FROM`,
which the cancel page needs to email its link; without them only a signed-in
owner of the address can cancel there. Checkout sells Premium only with

browser `billing.enabled`, `billing.cancellable`, `billing.cancelByEmail`,
`billing.launchOffer` and `billing.seller` (`link` under Managed Payments,
`trader` with plain Stripe, `null` until both the key and
`STRIPE_MANAGED_PAYMENTS` are set). The portal, the webhook and the cancel
page need only the key, so subscribers keep them after sales are switched
off. With billing off the locks still apply.

## Coins

The server token ledger of migration 028 is the wallet; the UI calls it **Coins**
and the code keeps its `token` identifiers (tables, ops, `shared/rewards.ts`).
Do not rename the schema. The browser keeps no coin balance of its own; old
`localStorage` numbers stay in the account blob unread.

- **Rules.** `shared/rewards.ts` holds the defaults (`DEFAULT_COIN_SETTINGS`)
  and the game settings mirror them as `coins`, clamped on read, so the owner
  tunes them in `/dev` → Settings → Coins without a deploy. Every account
  earns 10 % of verified XP; Premium doubles it at credit time; one account
  earns at most 400 coins a day from XP, counted after the doubling (4,000 XP
  a day on Free, 2,000 on Premium). The welcome grant is 200. Premium
  milestones: a live streak of 7, 30 and 100 days (25, 100, 300; "live" as the
  Profile counts it, `streak_live`), a Learn topic with every level passed
  (100), an evolving project (150) or short path (50) with every stage passed,
  and the top three of a finished calendar month on the "This month" board
  (300, 200, 100). A tie shares the place: everyone tied gets that place's
  coins and the next total takes the place after the tied group (1, 1, 3), so
  nobody below third is paid. Milestones sit outside the daily cap. Opening a
  social profile pays nothing; the `social` ledger reason stays for credits
  already made.
- **Credits.** Every credit is a ledger event with a deterministic id, written
  by a service-role routine, so a replay credits nothing:
  `credit_verified_xp_tokens` (`xp:<award id>`, with `token_xp_credits`
  recording each decision, a capped one included), `settle_coin_milestones`
  (`streak:<n>:<account>`, `topic:<id>:<account>`, `project:<id>:<account>`),
  `settle_month_top3` (`month-top:<yyyy-mm>:<place>:<account>`, once per month
  in `token_month_settlements`), `grant_signup_tokens` (`signup:<account>`) and
  `credit_social_visit` (`social:<platform>:<account>`). The routines read
  `is_premium()` themselves; none writes a learning, score or streak table.
  `settle_coin_milestones` asks `is_premium()` when it settles, so a new
  subscriber's first wallet read pays milestones reached on the free plan.
  When a subscription is refunded, withdrawn or disputed,
  `revoke_premium_benefits` debits that grant's milestone and doubled coins
  once (`revoke:<grant>`, reason `adjustment`) and cancels its unsent
  redemptions; a milestone taken back stays in the ledger, so it is never paid
  twice.
- **Where credits happen.** `lib/rewards/coins.ts` is the server side. A quiz
  or daily result and a Biggest Shark Challenge run credit the XP the routine
  awarded, and nothing for a refused second daily. A Learn level or part test
  passed for the first time credits `learn:<account>:<topic>:L<n>` (or `P<n>`)
  at 50 × the level's difficulty or 300 × the part (`shared/progression.ts`); a
  coding task's first pass credits `coding:<account>:<task>`, the same id its
  XP uses. Repeat XP (a coding task after a reset, a Learn step after an hour)
  credits no coins. Everything that moves the streak (a verified quiz, a
  completed Learn level or part test, an applied coding pass, an awarded
  Challenge run) then settles the milestones, so a streak milestone reached on
  a Learn or coding day pays at once. The first wallet read (`op=wallet` GET)
  pays the welcome coins, settles the month that just ended and the learner's
  milestones, and returns the rules and progress for "How to earn".
- **Spending.** The crown (1,200) and streak protection (250) are open to
  every account. Redeeming coins for shipped merchandise is a Premium benefit
  (`op=orders` POST answers a free account with 402 `premium_required`, `kind:
  'merch-redemption'`), and merchandise is paused ("Merchandise").
  `merch_stock` is a monthly cap on what the owner will post, not a count of
  goods on a shelf.
- **Screen.** `/shop` reads Coins in the navigation and in its title, with a
  coin as its header icon: the coins with the last 25 ledger lines, How to
  earn (five rows and one Premium note), the crown and streak protection,
  Invite a friend, and, once merchandise returns, merchandise and orders.
  devShark's own social profiles are linked once, as icons in the site footer,
  from `SOCIAL_PROFILES` in `client/product-catalog.ts`, and show only once
  the owner records them.
- **Invitations (migration 042).** devShark's own server verifies the event
  it pays for. Every account has an eight-letter code (`referral_codes`), and
  its invite link is `/?ref=<code>`. `client/src/main.tsx` keeps the code in
  local storage and takes it out of the address bar; after sign-in
  `ReferralBinder` offers it to `op=referral` POST. `record_referral` binds it
  once (`referrals`, keyed by the invited account), only while the account is
  at most 48 hours old by the creation time Supabase Auth reported for the
  verified token, and refuses the account's own code or a pair that already
  runs the other way. When the invited friend has a passed Learn level,
  `credit_referral` pays both sides `referralGrant` coins (100) once: the
  friend under `referral:<account>`, the inviter under
  `referral:friend:<random key>`, so the inviter's ledger never carries the
  friend's id. An inviter is paid for at most `referralCap` friends (20),
  counted from the inviter's own ledger; past it the friend is still paid. A
  first Learn pass and the wallet read settle it. The routine writes the
  ledger and the referral row and nothing else. `op=referral` GET returns the
  code and counts only; `referrals` has no browser policy. A grant of 0 in
  `/dev` turns invitations off.
- **Merchandise through Spreadshop (migration 043).** Spreadshop (sprd.net
  AG) prints, sells and ships devShark merchandise and has no order API, so
  the app links out and embeds nothing: no Spreadshop script, iframe or CSP
  entry. `MERCH_SHOP` in `client/product-catalog.ts` holds the shop's URL and
  one URL per item, all null until the owner sets them; a tile shows "Buy at
  the devShark shop" only for a set URL, and a mockup only when the build
  finds `client/public/merch/<sku>.<ext>` (`vite.config.ts` lists them). Cash
  is paid in the Spreadshop checkout and `cashCheckoutEnabled` stays off. A
  coin redemption or a claimed learning-path package is a `merch_orders` row;
  the owner orders it at base price from the shop preview and works the queue
  in `/dev` → Merchandise, which also edits the quotes and the monthly caps
  (`op=fulfilment`, `set_merch_stock`). A claimed package ships without
  touching `reserved`. `lib/rewards/spreadshop.ts` reads Spreadshop's own
  monthly promotion on the server when `SPREADSHOP_API_KEY` and
  `SPREADSHOP_SHOP_ID` are set, and `/api/settings` returns it as
  `merchPromo`; coins never buy a discount. All of this is behind
  `MERCH_ENABLED`.

## Sign-in, caching and limits

- **Two ways in.** Every "Log in" and "Sign
  in" opens one dialog (`openSignIn` in `client/src/lib/signInDialog.ts`,
  rendered by `components/auth/SignInDialogHost.tsx` in the shell, its code
  loaded on the first press like the upgrade sheet's). "Continue with
  Google" comes first; under an "or" an email and password form signs in,
  creates an account, or sends a reset link (`client/src/lib/emailAuth.ts`).
  A page that names a return path (the checkout pages, the voucher) passes
  it in; Google's round trip and an email sign-in both land there through
  `lib/authReturn.ts`. New passwords need 8 characters and at most 72
  bytes (bcrypt's limit); signing in checks nothing but that a password was
  typed. Each Supabase refusal has its own sentence: wrong email or
  password, an unconfirmed address (with "Resend confirmation email"), an
  existing account (Supabase answers a sign-up for a confirmed address with
  a user without identities; the dialog offers sign-in or a reset), a weak
  or breached password, too many attempts or emails, email sign-in switched
  off, an unusable address, no connection, and a 5xx.
- **Confirmation.** Production confirms every new address
  (`mailer_autoconfirm=false`), so a sign-up signs nobody in: the dialog
  says where the link went and offers it again once Supabase's
  one-email-a-minute limit has passed. The link lands on
  `/auth/confirmed?next=<the page that asked>`; supabase-js reads the
  session from the address, the first sign-in runs as any other (the
  sign-in log, the welcome coins on the first wallet read, progress sync),
  and the page offers Continue to `next` (same-site paths only). An expired
  or used link says so and asks for a new one; signed in, it says there is
  nothing more to do. Until the address is confirmed Supabase refuses the
  password, so no unconfirmed session exists, and the server's address
  grants count only confirmed addresses anyway (below).
- **Forgotten password.** The reset link lands on `/reset-password` as a
  recovery session (supabase-js's `PASSWORD_RECOVERY`, kept as
  `passwordRecovery` in `lib/auth.tsx`). The page asks for the new password
  twice, sets it with `updateUser`, then ends the account's sessions on
  other devices (`signOut({ scope: 'others' })`). A reset link Supabase
  sends to the home page instead (an address missing from its allow-list)
  still opens the form (`lib/authLanding.ts`). Signed in, the page changes
  the password; signed out, it asks for a link and, like Supabase, never
  says whether the address has an account. A sign-in by reset link is
  logged like any sign-in.
- **Scanner-safe links.** Both pages also accept
  `?token_hash=…&type=email|signup` and `?token_hash=…&type=recovery`, the
  links the templates in `docs/auth-email-templates.md` send, and verify
  them only when the learner presses the button: a mail filter that fetches
  every link cannot use one up. Both pages are client routes behind the catch-all rewrite, kept out
  of search (`NOINDEX_PATHS`), with no handler of their own.
- **An account without Google.** It has no name or picture. Its header and
  Profile show the first part of its address. Friends and live rooms never
  show the address (they show its sharkname, "Learner", or "Player" and a
  number); the boards and Challenge scores show the sharkname unless the
  learner picks the account name, the confirmed address's local part
  ("Leaderboards"). Deleting it works as for any account: nothing in
  `delete-account` reads a Google identity (`npm run test:launch`,
  verified-caller suite).
- **The token on a request.** `apiFetch` (`client/src/lib/api.ts`) attaches
  the Supabase access token. When a session is stored in the browser but its
  token cannot be read within four seconds, or supabase-js answers no session
  while keeping the stored one (a refresh that failed offline), nothing is
  sent: the caller gets a retryable `ApiError(0, 'auth_unavailable')`. Only a
  browser with no stored session sends a request without a token, so a
  signed-in learner is never graded or refused as a guest. The one exception
  is the short list of public reads in `isPublicRead` (`/api/settings`, the
  Learn map, the path catalog and a lesson): their handlers never read the
  caller and the CDN shares their answers, so they leave at once without a
  token instead of waiting for supabase-js and the session (#587 C10). A
  handler that starts reading the caller must leave that list. Every other
  request of a returning learner carries the stored access token straight from
  localStorage while it is more than 90 seconds from its expiry
  (`freshStoredAccessToken`): supabase-js's `getSession()` hands out that same
  token unchanged inside that window (auth-js `EXPIRY_MARGIN_MS`), so the
  request no longer waits for the 54 KB supabase-js download. A token closer
  to its expiry, a malformed session, blocked storage or a page that opened on
  an OAuth return asks supabase-js as before, which refreshes or removes the
  session. The trade-off is bounded by the token's own expiry: the server
  verifies it on every request exactly as before.
- **The plan read** (`useEntitlement`, `lib/entitlement.ts`). A returning
  learner's plan is asked as the app starts (`prefetchStoredAccountPlan` in
  `main.tsx`), for the stored session's account and only with a token
  supabase-js would hand out unchanged, so a Learn map's first-data hold finds
  it answered instead of starting it after auth has restored the user (#587
  C14). It is the same request the header made anyway. A tab shown again
  re-reads the plan only once it is five minutes old (`PLAN_FOCUS_REFRESH_MS`;
  it was every return past the 60-second staleTime, an Auth `getUser` and an
  `entitlement_summary` read each, #587 C29). The plan still changes at once
  where it matters: the billing pages and the voucher write or invalidate it,
  every refused lock and upgrade sheet re-reads it, and a tab that reads a
  plan different from the last one this browser saw writes
  `devshark:plan-seen` (`<user id>:<tier>`, cleared with the account's data),
  which makes the other tabs re-read theirs. A change made on another device
  shows within five minutes or at the next lock pressed.
- **The sign-in report** (`POST /api/user/authevent`, the `/dev` Logs tab)
  goes once per sign-in. supabase-js also says `SIGNED_IN` for the stored
  session each time a tab is shown again; `lib/auth.tsx` remembers the
  sign-in its `INITIAL_SESSION` restored (the access token's `session_id`,
  which survives refreshes) and reports no `SIGNED_IN` for that same sign-in,
  so a new tab no longer sends a report on its first return (#587 C30). An
  OAuth return, a sign-in in the tab, a reset link and a session that
  replaces another are still reported.
- **Log out** ends this browser's session (`signOut({ scope: 'local' })`).
  Offline or on a 5xx, supabase-js keeps the session, so `lib/auth.tsx` removes
  the stored session and its code verifier itself, forgets the account's data
  and signs the page out. Signing out everywhere needs the server and reports a
  failure. `clearAccountData` empties the query cache before the next
  account's first render (some keys carry no account id), writes any draft still waiting
  (`beforeAccountChange`), then moves an account epoch (`accountEpoch`), and
  work that started before it (a progress sync, an XP sync, a level
  completion, a Challenge reward) writes and announces nothing.
- **Progress is read, never sent.** The server writes Learn progress itself on
  each completion. The browser pulls it at sign-in and when Today or a map
  asks (`GET /api/quiz/roadmap?resource=progress`, `syncProgressWithServer`,
  one request in flight per account) and sends nothing back; a PUT there
  answers 405. A sync that answered in the last minute
  (`PROGRESS_SYNC_FRESH_MS`) for the same account, with no level completed
  since and the device copy as it left it, answers the next ask, so the
  sign-in sync, Today and a Learn map share one read and a return to the map
  reads nothing new (#587 C08, C22). Changes made in this tab come back in the
  answer that made them; one made in another tab or on another device shows
  within the minute.
- **Caching.** `withRequestContext` sets `Cache-Control: private, no-store` on
  every answer before the handler runs, and the public reads set their own:
  the shared boards `public, s-maxage=60`; the Learn map `public, max-age=60,
  s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=300`, so the CDN shares it; a lesson
  `public, max-age=300, s-maxage=86400` with its ETag; the curation coverage
  `public, s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=300`; settings and the
  Challenge board `public, s-maxage=15, stale-while-revalidate=60`. Health
  answers `no-store`.
- **Grants by address.** The admin allow-list, the owner's granted Learn paths
  and the owner's private categories count an email only once Supabase has
  confirmed it (`confirmedEmail` in `lib/auth.ts`), as billing does. The admin
  role in `app_metadata` needs no email.
- **Read limits.** The wallet, badges, a friend lookup, the sharkname
  (`friends-handle`) and the board name (`leaderboard-name`) GETs in
  `api/user/[op].ts` and the Learn plan, progress and steps take `limitRead`
  (`lib/rate-limit.ts`): an address bucket that holds a class behind one NAT,
  then the verified account's own (120 a minute). A guest has the address
  bucket alone, which the Learn map and the leaderboard take too. The
  fulfilment op takes the admin bucket before its gate, GET included.
- **Class-sized writes.** `enforceClassRateLimit` takes an address bucket
  sized for a class behind one NAT, then the caller's own bucket (the
  verified account, or the address for a caller without one). Question
  reports use it (`questionReportAddress`, then `questionReport`: three, then
  one every three minutes), so a class can report more than three questions
  at once.
- **One-time claims.** `claimOnce` (one grade per placement round, one check
  per design walkthrough) is held in Upstash. Where Upstash is configured, a
  claim it cannot record is answered 503 `claim_unavailable` and nothing is
  graded; only without Upstash does an instance's memory hold it. A quiz
  submit's own claim (`claim_quiz_submission`) answers the same 503 when the
  database cannot take it.
- **Auth outages.** `requireAuth`, the sign-in report and the admin gate
  answer 503 `auth_unavailable` when Supabase Auth drops the connection
  (supabase-js `AuthRetryableFetchError`, status 0), throttles or sends an
  answer it cannot read, never 401 or 403, so a signed-in learner is not told
  their session ended. `lib/auth.ts` verifies a token at most once per request
  (`tryAuthOnce`), and admin writes log the admin account that made them
  (`by`).
- **Timeouts.** `withTimeout` (`lib/http.ts`) rejects a Supabase call that
  has not settled in time with `UpstreamTimeoutError`, a
  `ServiceUnavailableError`, so the caller gets 503 `upstream_timeout`, which
  it may retry, instead of 500. `withRequestContext` logs every 503 at level
  error, and an escaped `AuthError` with the 401 or 503 it answers.
- **Text with U+0000.** Postgres text and JSONB cannot store it, so a coding
  draft, a learning-path draft or artifact, a Shark Card and a question
  report holding it answer 400 before any write.
- **Game settings.** A failed settings read serves the instance's last known
  settings, or the defaults when it has none, and logs the failure, so a
  database blip never turns back on a feature the owner switched off.

## Account erasure

`DELETE /api/user/delete-account` ends any live Stripe subscription first and
stops with 503 if Stripe cannot be reached. A database call that times out
answers 503 `upstream_timeout`, and any other failure 500. It then calls `delete_user_data`,
the one routine that erases every table holding an account id (restated last
in `supabase/supabase-schema-060.sql`, which covers the voucher redemptions,
merchandise, the month's XP ledger `user_xp_days`, the question hours
`user_question_xp` and the Learn step hours `user_learn_step_xp`). Tables that
reference an erased table with `ON DELETE CASCADE` go with it, as
`shark_name_allocations` goes with `user_handles`.

An order Spreadshop never received gives its stock reservation back (a
claimed package reserved none): one awaiting payment is deleted, and a paid
one is cancelled, which takes it off the fulfilment queue, and kept without
the person as the record of what was paid. A few rows stay without the
person: those cancelled orders, a merchandise order already with Spreadshop
and its package claim (the claim's account part becomes
`deleted-account:<order id>`), a settled month's ranks, a referral the
account made (`deleted-account`), and a voucher the account created as an
admin, which keeps its counts (`created_by` becomes `deleted-account`).

The handler calls no other routine. After the Auth identity is deleted it
calls `delete_user_data` once more, because the webhook of the subscription it
cancelled can write billing rows in between; once the identity is gone,
`link_billing_customer` and `upsert_provider_entitlement` refuse the id.
`erasureContracts()` in `scripts/test-launch-contracts.ts` fails when a
migration creates a table with an account column that the newest
`delete_user_data` does not erase, and `supabase/tests/080-delete-user-data.test.sql`
seeds a row in every such table.

## Cookie consent, analytics and the privacy policy

- **The choice.** `client/src/lib/consent.ts` holds three categories:
  `necessary` (always on: sign-in, settings, progress kept in the browser, the
  choice itself), `analytics` (PostHog) and `marketing` (GA4 and the Meta
  Pixel, not connected). The choice is stored in localStorage
  (`devshark:consent`: version, the two booleans, the time) and copied into
  the first-party cookie `devshark_consent`
  (`v=1&analytics=0|1&marketing=0|1&at=<epoch s>`, `SameSite=Lax`, 12
  months) for a later server or edge read; nothing reads the cookie today.
  Until the visitor decides, both optional categories are off. Raising
  `CONSENT_VERSION` asks every browser again, and so does a choice older than
  12 months. A browser that refuses storage counts as not consented: the
  banner closes for the page, nothing optional runs, and the next visit asks
  again. A choice made in another tab applies through the `storage` event.
- **The banner and the dialog** (`client/src/components/CookieConsent.tsx`,
  `CookieConsentDialog.tsx`). The shell mounts the banner right before
  `<main>`, which the page focuses on load, so one Shift+Tab reaches it and a
  screen reader reads it before the page; CSS (`order`) draws it under
  `<main>` and 30px above the bottom edge, so `<main>` gives up the height and
  nothing is covered. Accept all, Reject all and Choose are the
  same Astryx secondary button, 44px tall. On `/privacy` and `/terms` the
  banner shows its title and actions only. It is hidden where the shell hides
  its chrome (`/dev`, a running quiz). Choose and the footer's "Cookie
  settings" button (`openConsentSettings`) open one Astryx dialog, whose code
  is a chunk of its own (fetched in the background while the banner shows; a
  failed load closes with a toast and the next press asks again): a switch
  per category with its purpose and a link to the policy section, Necessary
  locked on, Reject all, Accept all and Save choices. The native modal makes
  the page inert, `useFocusTrap` wraps Tab, Escape closes, and focus returns
  to the opener or to `<main>` when the opener left with the banner. The
  privacy policy's cookie section has the same button. While the banner
  shows, `--ss-consent-dock` lifts `AppToast` and `XpToaster` above it.
- **PostHog** (`client/src/lib/analytics.ts`). `initAnalytics()` follows the
  consent store. posthog-js, its settings and the URL scrubber live in
  `lib/posthogClient.ts`, which only a yes imports. Without a yes, posthog-js
  is not downloaded and nothing is
  stored or sent; the current path and the signed-in id wait in memory and
  go out after a yes on the same page, and the first-touch campaign
  (`devshark:campaign`) is written only then. PostHog is initialised with
  `opt_out_capturing_by_default` and `opt_out_persistence_by_default` and
  opted in explicitly, because it reads its own consent flag from storage on
  every check. A no or a withdrawal calls `reset()` then
  `opt_out_capturing()` (in that order: `reset()` clears PostHog's flag) and
  removes every `ph_` / `__ph_` cookie and storage key and the campaign,
  whenever they were written. Sign-out calls `reset()` and opts back in.
  `before_send` scrubs URLs before anything leaves the browser.
- **Marketing readiness** (`client/src/lib/marketing.ts`). `MARKETING_LOADERS`
  (`lib/marketingTags.ts`) is empty, so `main.tsx` never loads `marketing.ts`
  and nothing runs: no `dataLayer`, no script, no CSP change. A loader added
  there starts only after a yes to marketing,
  behind Google Consent Mode v2 defaults (`ad_storage`, `analytics_storage`,
  `ad_user_data`, `ad_personalization` denied; a yes grants all four, since
  GA4 sits under marketing). Connecting a tag also needs its hosts in the CSP
  of `vercel.json`, a raised `CONSENT_VERSION` and the policy's marketing
  section and cookie table updated.
- **Sentry** stays under necessary because it stores nothing in the browser
  and sends no personal data: no cookie, no storage (`linkPreviousTrace`
  pinned to `in-memory`), `sendDefaultPii: false`, no Session Replay, no
  `setUser`. Its scrub hooks (in the lazy `client/src/lib/sentry-sdk.ts`)
  remove query strings, fragments, e-mail addresses, tokens, request data and
  the user from every error, breadcrumb and trace. Adding any of those moves
  it under analytics consent. The SDK chunk loads after the page's load event,
  at the browser's next idle moment, or at once on the first error (#587 C09);
  an error reported or thrown before that waits in memory (at most 20) and is
  sent through the same scrub hooks when the SDK starts. The pageload trace
  still reads its timings from the browser's buffered performance entries.
  PostHog sends each event as it is captured, so
  nothing batched before a withdrawal leaves after it.
- **The privacy policy** (`PRIVACY` in `client/src/components/LegalPages.tsx`,
  keys `legal.privacy.*` in `client/src/i18n/translations.privacy.ts`, which
  only the legal pages' chunk imports, so the policy stays out of the shell's
  bundle; LegalPages looks a key up there first and in `translations.ts`
  otherwise) covers sign-in (Google or email and password),
  learning data and Shark Cards, the sharkname and what friends see, the
  boards, the Hall of Fame, Play rooms, invitations, reports, payments,
  vouchers, merchandise (not offered), email, cookies with a table of every
  name, analytics, marketing, error monitoring, providers and transfers, legal
  bases, a retention table, deletion, rights and changes. The cookie table
  (`COOKIE_ROWS`) names the real keys; change it with them. The sign-in log
  (`auth_events`) keeps a record 12 months: migration 057 adds that delete to
  `purge_expired_learning_data`, which production runs daily through
  pg_cron, and `consentContracts()` in `scripts/test-launch-contracts.ts`
  ties the sentence to the SQL.

## Client start-up

What every page pays before its own content, and the rules that keep it
small (perf audit #587). The sign-in, token and plan rules are under
"Sign-in, caching and limits"; error monitoring under "Cookie consent,
analytics and the privacy policy".

- **The first page.** The route box's Suspense boundary (`App.tsx`) draws
  the loader as its content on the first commit, and the first page follows
  in a transition (`useFirstPageTransition`, `client/src/lib/routePreload.ts`),
  as every later navigation already did. React commits a boundary that leaves
  its fallback on a retry no sooner than 300 ms after the last fallback any
  boundary showed or hid (the header's account widget among them), so the
  first page of a visit used to wait up to 300 ms with its code and data in
  hand (#587 C04). What shows is unchanged: nothing for 200 ms, then the
  loader, then the page. `main.tsx` starts the first page's chunk
  (`preloadPath`) before anything renders, so the transition finds the import
  already running.
- **The document head.** `lib/routeHead.ts` (titles, descriptions,
  canonical links, structured data), with the public pages' metadata and the
  topic catalogue it reads, is its own chunk: `App.tsx` starts it as it
  evaluates and writes each route's head when it has arrived, the latest
  route only. It was about 3 KB gzip of the first download. The share head a
  coding task or a dated question of the day arrived with is read as the
  chunk arrives, before any route writes; a failed load is asked again on the
  next navigation.
- **Motion.** `MotionProvider` (`client/src/lib/motion.tsx`) loads Motion's
  feature chunk (14 KB) in the background. A page that opens with
  `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` loads none (`motionFeaturesFor`) and marks
  `<html data-motion="off">`, the mark a failed load sets, so app-shell.css
  shows each `m` element at its final opacity without a transform and an
  exiting one leaves at once: such a reader sees things appear instead of a
  120 ms fade. A preference changed while the page is open applies from the
  next load.

## Compatibility and rollback

Records from the DevQuiz era are `webdev`: migration 022 assigns that subject
to legacy daily and challenge rows and keeps legacy total XP beside the
subject map. Server-verified receipts and attempts make progress idempotent.
Migrations only move forward. Rolling application code back leaves every
applied migration in place: their new columns are backward-compatible, their
revoked browser writes are security controls. Where a newer routine is
missing, a handler either falls back to the older one or refuses with 503
`migration_required` (`npm run test:fallbacks`).

## No AI features

devShark ships no AI feature. There is no AI provider, and the quiz submit
handler routes no explanation or hint resource; the launch contracts assert
both. Coding-challenge hints are authored content whose last rung before the
reference solution is a documentation link (`shared/coding-docs.ts`).

Merchandise is paused (owner decision, 1 October 2026). No package has

## Shark Cards

Shark Cards are saved questions that explain their topic. The collectible
card packs are retired: `op=cards` answers 410 `gone`, and nothing grants
cards. `user_cards` and its rows stay, and `delete_user_data` still erases
them (SQL test 200).

- **Saving.** Wherever a graded explanation is shown (the quiz and daily
  challenge review, the question of the day, a wrong Learn answer, and the
  Challenge's game-over list of the run's misses), a signed-in learner gets
  "Save as Shark Card" (`SaveSharkCard.tsx`). It saves a `flashcards` row
  through `/api/flashcards`: the question, its topic, and the correct option
  and explanation the grading of the learner's own answer returned. Pressed
  again, it takes the card out. The button knows a card is saved from the
  deck when this visit read it, else from this device's bookmark list.
- **No answer leaks.** `/api/flashcards` stores what the learner was shown
  after grading and reads the bank only to check the question belongs to the
  subject and to take its category. It never reads an answer or an
  explanation from the bank, so saving a card for an unanswered question
  reveals nothing, and every read, write and delete is filtered to the
  caller's own rows. `scripts/product-cleanup-contracts.ts` holds both.
- **Review.** `/collection` opens on the Shark Cards tab (saved coding
  challenges are the second tab), and `/cards` shows the same deck on its
  own. One card at a time: the front is the question and its topic; "Show
  the answer" turns it to the correct answer, the explanation and "Learn this
  topic", which goes to the question's own Learn level (`rm-<topic>-<n>` ids,
  eight per level), else the topic in Learn, else a quiz on a topic Learn does
  not teach (`sharkCardStudyLink`). Previous and Next (and ← →) move through
  the deck; "Got it" takes the card out with an Undo. Empty, signed-out,
  loading and error states say how to get cards.

## Marketing source export

`GET /api/quiz/questions?resource=marketing-source` (`lib/marketing/source.ts`)
exports bounded `marketing-source/1` packets for OwnDashboard, the owner's
separate marketing tool: questions with their authored answers and
allowlisted prompts and starters of active coding tasks, behind the current
curation gate and fresh question overrides. It reads no learner records and
makes no model or publishing calls. A failed, truncated or changing override
read holds the export, a retained invalid item yields a tombstone, and a hard
removal answers 410 on a recheck. Dated daily packets reuse the platform
selector and bind their revision to the real `/daily/<date>` link.

The export is inactive. It needs a bearer secret of at least 32 characters in
`DEVSHARK_MARKETING_EXPORT_SECRET`, which no Vercel environment sets, so the
operation answers 503 `marketing_not_configured`. `npm run test:marketing`
(part of `test:launch`) keeps the contract proven. Nothing in this repository
posts to social networks. See
[marketing-social-bridge.md](marketing-social-bridge.md) for the header
contract and limits.
